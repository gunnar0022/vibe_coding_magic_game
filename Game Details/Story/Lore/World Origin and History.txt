WORLD ORIGIN AND HISTORY
========================


THE EVENT: THE DISCOVERY OF MAGIC
----------------------------------

Magic is not ancient. It is a recent phenomenon that manifested suddenly and globally. Its emergence was instantaneous, catastrophic, and irreversible. When magic entered the world, it fundamentally altered reality in a way that rendered all modern technology inert. Devices failed instantly and permanently. Infrastructure collapsed overnight. Entire systems of production, communication, transportation, and medicine ceased to function.

This event did not resemble a slow decline. It was an abrupt severing of the world from its technological foundations. Society was not prepared, trained, or culturally equipped to survive without them.

Many people died. Others fled urban centers, abandoning cities in favor of wild or semi-wild regions where food, water, and shelter could be secured directly.


HOW MAGIC WAS FIRST UNDERSTOOD
-------------------------------

For a long time after the collapse, most people did not recognize what had happened as "magic" at all. The world broke too quickly and too completely. Technology failed, ecosystems shifted, and survival became the only priority. There was no language, framework, or distance to attribute these changes to a single underlying cause. To most, the catastrophe simply was.

Only later -- after the world had already begun reshaping itself -- did a small number of people experience visions. These individuals did not understand what they were seeing, but through them it became possible, for the first time, to intentionally interact with the unseen force saturating the world. These encounters were not teachings, but resonances: brief moments where the latent magic of the world aligned with human perception and revealed a fragment of information. Each such individual received only a single symbol, often at great personal cost.

Through these unstable connections, humanity gained its first foothold in learning how to harness magic deliberately. The earliest users, however, were unskilled and unprepared. Many were exposed through fragmented visions or incomplete understanding, unaware of the risks involved.


THE NATURE OF MAGIC
--------------------

Magic itself is not passive. It appears to have organized spontaneously after the collapse into something resembling a primitive form of life -- parallel to consciousness, but entirely unlike human thought or intention. It is likely not intelligent in any meaningful sense. It does not reason, judge, or plan. It responds. When invoked, it amplifies what already exists within living things, pushing them toward their natural extremes. This amplification is neither benevolent nor malicious, but it is unforgiving.

Discovery is ongoing and global. New symbols, creatures, interactions, and applications of magic continue to surface across the world. No single community possesses a complete understanding. Knowledge is fragmented, localized, and often contradictory -- what one village treats as foundational truth may be dismissed as superstition by another. Magic is real, tangible, and undeniable, yet its nature remains elusive.

No one knows where magic originated or why it manifests through symbol-like forms. These symbols resemble written characters, but their source is unknown and fiercely debated. Some believe they are tools for focusing energy. Others believe they are natural imprints left by the world itself. There is no unified theory, and disagreement is common, especially regarding more abstract symbols.


THE HOLLOWING: HOW PEOPLE BREAK
----------------------------------

Magic itself is not the poison. Every area in the world contains a large, shared pool of environmental mana, generated by the life in that region. Using magic from a full environment is safe. The world gives freely when it has something to give. The pool is finite but regenerates -- life breathes it back. The land always recovers.

The danger begins when someone keeps taking after there is nothing left. When the environmental pool is drained and a person continues casting, they draw from themselves -- their health, their being. Each time this happens, it carves a gap in the soul. That gap is filled with emptiness. And that emptiness gets filled by whatever is loudest inside the person -- grief, pride, hunger, fear, wrath, ambition. Magic does not inject corruption. It creates the void. A person's own nature fills it.

The process is gradual and invisible. A person does not feel it happening. They simply become more of what they already were, and less of everything else. This is the hollowing.

The complexity of the transformation mirrors the complexity of the motive that fills the void. A desperate, hungry group that hollowed themselves out trying to survive becomes something simple -- feral humanoid creatures with one drive, one purpose, stripped of everything else. Bestial. Easy to recognize. Openly hostile. These are the remnants of simple desperation.

A person carrying grief, pride, protectiveness, wrath, and genuine love -- if they hollow enough of themselves out, those drives expand to fill the space but retain their complexity. The result can still think. Still plan. Still feel twisted versions of what it felt before. The most dangerous corrupted beings in this world are the ones that can still talk to you.

In hindsight, some of the earliest transformations may not have been acts of malice, but of misfortune. Early adopters lacked the understanding to recognize what was happening. They kept taking from themselves because they did not know the cost. Later, as word spread that this mysterious force could be engaged with, belief systems proliferated rapidly. Different groups interpreted magic in radically different ways. Some viewed it as a tool. Others feared it as a curse. These diverging interpretations, combined with uneven access and understanding, contributed to further instability.

The existence of hollowed creatures -- and the understanding that they were once people -- led to the rise of new belief systems and religions. Many attempt to resurrect moral frameworks from the old world, not out of nostalgia, but as a form of restraint -- a defense against repeating the choices that birthed such horrors.

The world itself is regenerative. There is no spreading darkness across the land. Drained areas recover as life breathes mana back. The land always heals. The danger is entirely behavioral. Settlements do not fall because corruption spread to them -- they fall because people made choices under pressure and refused to stop taking.


ECOLOGICAL ACCELERATION
------------------------

Alongside technological collapse, magic accelerated cycles of decay, growth, and rebirth. The reason would not be known immediately, but it would soon become apparent that something had changed in the way plants behaved. What exactly that thing was wouldn't be known for a long time, and those who wondered couldn't afford to spend much time pondering as there were much more pressing matters at hand.

Plant life reclaimed abandoned structures at an unnatural pace. Concrete fractured, metal corroded, and ruins were consumed by living systems within decades rather than centuries. They would eventually come to realize that something akin to "magic" as described in old fantasy stories was to blame.

This rapid reclamation erased much physical evidence of the old world. What remains is fragmentary, degraded, and often dismissed as myth or misunderstanding. Most people are too occupied with survival and stability to investigate the past.

Nature itself has not been spared transformation. Predators have become more efficient, clever, and dangerous. Prey has adapted in response, developing new behaviors and survival mechanisms. Plant life has flourished under the influence of magic, becoming abundant, aggressive, reactive, or strangely generous depending on its environment. The world is alive in a way it never was before.


TEMPORAL SETTING
-----------------

The game takes place two to three generations after the event. Elders may remember being children during the collapse, but few understood what was happening at the time. Practical application of magic did not exist during the event itself.

By the time of the game, the world has just about stabilized into a new equilibrium. Loose villages, isolated communities, and small factions dominate the landscape. Long-distance communication is rare and unreliable. Travel is dangerous and uncommon. Many are not even certain where their nearest neighbors are. During the collapse, the mentality was that your community must be preserved and relied on at all costs, or it would mean death. Strangers were not to be trusted. Such was the reality of a desperate humanity.


THE PLAYER
-----------

The player was born into this world. Magic is not miraculous or feared -- it is simply normal, part of the landscape. They grew up in a humble village of about fifteen families. No currency, only cooperation. A central plaza where people eat together. Small, functional, home.

The player is not a chosen hero, but someone shaped by loss and forced into choice. An attack by hollowed creatures -- feral, corrupted humans from a failed nearby settlement -- killed the player's parents and childhood friend, and wounded the village deeply. The player's older brother saved their life during the attack.

In the aftermath, the brother cannot stay. The village is a wound he has to walk away from. He wants the player to come with him. He leaves regardless. The brother's departure -- not the attack -- is the true inciting incident. The attack creates the conditions. His leaving creates the choice.

The player is left in a damaged but living village, pulled in multiple directions by people with their own responses to what happened: pursue power, seek understanding, rebuild home, or let go and wander. The core emotional question of the game is: "What do you do with loss?"

This is a post-apocalyptic world defined not by scarcity, but by abundance. Life thrives, but it does so violently and unevenly. Survival alone does not guarantee safety. Even generations after the collapse, the world remains dangerous, unstable, and urgent.

Humanity did not simply survive the end of the old world. It is still learning how to exist in the new one.
