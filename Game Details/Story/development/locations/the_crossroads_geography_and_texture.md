# The Crossroads: Geography and Texture

## Location

**Name:** The Crossroads (no local name; everyone calls it by its function)

**Geography:** A river crossing in foothills where three valley routes converge. Not a town. Not controlled. Just where the land funnels people together.

The river is wide and swift at this point — unfordable except here, where an old stone bridge (pre-collapse construction, still standing) spans it. The bridge is the centerpiece. On the far bank, the three valleys open up: one leads north toward mountain settlements, one southwest toward coastal communities, one east toward the plains.

The crossing itself sits in a natural amphitheater of low hills. Enough space for encampments. Not enough to sustain a permanent settlement — water runs seasonal at the upstream source, the ground is rocky and poor for farming, winter cold would be brutal to endure long-term.

## Physical Character

**Before the gathering season:** Empty. The bridge stands alone. The hills are quiet. There's a rough stone shelter (pre-collapse, half-collapsed) near the bridge's western approach — enough to keep rain off, not enough to live in.

**During gathering season (varies by region, but roughly monthly or seasonal):** The Crossroads transforms.

Encampments appear. Traveling traders erect temporary structures — cloth tents, wooden frames lashed together, some semi-permanent buildings that are torn down after each gathering. The ground fills with people, animals (horses, goats, sometimes domesticated deer), fires.

It's chaotic. Dozens of communities represented, each with their own goods, needs, information. A de facto bazaar that exists for a week or two, then disperses.

**Aesthetics:**
- The stone bridge is beautiful in a functional way. Weathered. Moss and lichen on the northern face. The blocks are fitted with precision that speaks of time before the collapse.
- The water below is clear and fast, loud. The sound is constant, creates a baseline hum.
- The hills are bare or sparse vegetation — wind-scoured, not forest. Open sight lines. No place to hide, which means no bandits operate here (too exposed, too many witnesses).
- At night, fires dot the hills. During the day, smoke rises.
- Air smells like woodsmoke, animal dung, cooking food, leather, earth.

## How It Functions

The Crossroads has no authority. No one "runs" it. It exists because geography makes it inevitable.

Communities send representatives with goods to trade:
- Harvest surplus (grain, vegetables, preserved meat)
- Craft goods (cloth, pottery, tools, weapons)
- Information (news from other settlements, warnings about depleted mana zones, rumors about strange sightings)

Resources are exchanged through barter or (rarely) shared pooling for communal supplies. Alliances form. Relationships develop. Some people find mates. Some find new homes.

But it's also a place of **limited safety**. No formal law, so disputes are handled locally. Strong personalities dominate. Theft happens. Occasionally violence breaks out — fights are separated by bystanders, not judges. If someone injures another, they're expected to pay restitution or leave immediately.

The Crossroads survives by balance: enough people that chaos is discouraged by numbers, not enough authority that anyone feels trapped.

## Timing and Seasonality

The gathering season isn't fixed. Depends on what's been happening:
- If mana is abundant and travel is safe, gatherings happen monthly
- If mana is depleted or corruption is active, gatherings happen less frequently or move locations
- Winter disrupts travel (mountains close), so the Crossroads might be empty for months
- Spring reopens the routes, and communities converge again

**No calendar governs this.** It's organic. A settlement sends a scout who announces "we're coming in two weeks" to neighboring villages. Word spreads. People prepare. The gathering happens or doesn't depending on a hundred small decisions.

## Specific Encounters Per Path

### Knowledge Path
- The mentor researcher may have set up a *semi-permanent* camp here — not a building, but a recognizable spot where scholars gather
- The companion researcher knows where the mentor will be and when
- The player encounters other researchers, people interested in magical theory, people sharing findings
- The mentor has built a reputation; people seek him out

### Power/Community Paths
- Crossroads is a trading hub where goods are exchanged
- Power-path players might encounter adventurers, hunters, mercenaries passing through
- Community-path players encounter craftspeople, farmers, people organizing collective projects
- On community path specifically, the village might have a designated "representative" who comes to trade

### Wandering Path
- The Crossroads is a rest stop, not a destination
- The wanderer-mentor is sometimes here, sometimes not — they don't belong to any community, so they don't follow the gathering season
- The player encounters other wanderers, nomads, people in transit

### All Paths
- **The brother passes through** at least once, possibly multiple times depending on path choices
- His presence is memorable but brief — he doesn't stay. He trades for supplies, asks sharp questions (about corrupted sightings, mana depletion, the direction of settlements), and moves on
- On active paths where the player walked beside him, they might encounter him here
- On passive paths, the player might hear rumors ("An intense guy came through, asked about hollowed creatures")

## Notes for Future Sessions

- The Crossroads is **neutral ground**. This matters thematically. It's a place where the four paths can intersect without forced convergence. Each player experiences it differently.
- The stone bridge is pre-collapse infrastructure. It's a symbol of the old world still holding up. On active paths, the player might think "I could destroy this and cut off the southern route" (power fantasy). On passive paths, they might think "this bridge connects us all" (connection).
- The brother's passage through here can be developed later. He's present as a possibility, not yet a scene.
- Winter closure of the Crossroads creates natural pacing — travel gets blocked, communities isolate, the game's world rhythm matches seasonal cycles.
