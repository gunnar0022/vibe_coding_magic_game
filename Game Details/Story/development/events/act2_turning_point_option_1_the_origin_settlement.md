# Act 2 Turning Point — Option 1: The Origin Settlement Discovery

## Concept

The player discovers the settlement where the village attackers came from. Not a dramatic quest. Not a major narrative event. Just... finding it. Walking through the ruins. Understanding, step by step, that these creatures were people. That desperation hollowed them out. That the thing that attacked the village was the end result of choices made under pressure.

The recontextualization happens not through exposition, but through environmental storytelling and inference.

## The Discovery

**Trigger:** Varies by path, but something natural prompts exploration toward the origin settlement:
- **Power path:** Tracking corrupted beings to understand their pattern
- **Knowledge path:** Research hints at a nearby failed settlement
- **Community path:** Refugees or travelers mention where the attackers came from
- **Wandering path:** The player simply wanders and finds it by accident

**The Settlement Itself:**

A community similar to the player's village. Maybe fifteen families. Same scale. Same structure. But *empty*.

The buildings are intact — no violence here. No signs of attack. Just... abandoned. Recently. Within the past few months.

**Environmental Storytelling (Beat by Beat):**

1. **The first structure:** A family dwelling. Inside, signs of everyday life interrupted. A pot of food gone moldy. Clothes folded on a bed, never worn. A child's drawing on bark (stick figures, a sun, simple joy). The drawing is dated three months ago.

2. **The central plaza:** Similar to the player's village. A gathering space. Here, something different. Scratch marks on the ground where people gathered repeatedly. Deep marks, as if people stood in one place for a long time, shifting their weight. Deliberating. Deciding something.

3. **The storage buildings:** Empty. Not just "eaten," but *thoroughly* ransacked. Shelves pulled down. The ground scattered with spoiled grain husks, rotted root vegetables. Someone was looking for food. Desperately. Found nothing.

4. **A personal discovery:** Maybe a journal. Maybe a series of tally marks on a cave wall (counting days?). Maybe a letter, half-written, abandoned. Something that hints at *what was happening*.

Example journal entry:
> "We've been drawing from our own stores for three weeks. The mana is gone from this valley. Thorne says we should move, find a new place. But where? Everyone is in the same situation. The southern valleys are already taken. We can't leave. We have to wait. We have to try. I cast again today even though there was nothing left to give. I felt something break inside. Not painful. Just... empty."

5. **The departure scene:** Signs that people left suddenly, but not in panic. Deliberately. Organized. Their tracks head toward the player's village. They took what little they had. They marched as a group.

6. **The final piece:** Maybe a body. Or maybe just clothes, abandoned on the path as if someone shed their humanity like clothing. Not gruesome — just *wrong*. The implication is clearer than any explicit description: these people hollowed themselves out so completely that what's left isn't human anymore.

## What the Player Understands

Gradually, without exposition:
- The attackers were people with a home, a community, a shared life
- They didn't choose violence because they were evil
- They chose it because they were *desperate*
- Desperation and hollowing are connected
- The brother is heading in the same direction — toward desperation, toward the same choices

## The Brother's Role (Optional — Can Be Incorporated)

**If on active path:** The player finds evidence that the brother passed through here recently. A campfire still warm. A mark carved in a tree (his mark). He saw what happened here too. He understood. And he kept going anyway.

**Emotional implication:** The brother saw the same evidence. He *knows* what hollowing does. And he's still pursuing it. The player realizes the brother isn't making this choice from ignorance — he's making it consciously.

This moment can carry the "brother does something scary" weight: he's not a victim of circumstance. He's choosing power despite knowing the cost.

## Strengths of This Option

- **Environmental storytelling.** No exposition dump. The player infers the truth from evidence.
- **Universal understanding.** Works on all paths. Every player discovers the same place and makes the same realization.
- **Thematic resonance.** The hollow village mirrors the hollow people. The structure and emptiness reinforce each other.
- **Sets up attacker_origin_settlement discovery as a quest outcome** — if the player pursues it, they can find the settlement and report back to the village.
- **Carries the "they were people" requirement.** The journal entries, the child's drawing, the abandoned clothing — these are *specific people*, not abstractions.

## Weaknesses

- Requires environmental design/development to land emotionally. Can't be rushed.
- Doesn't provide *personal* understanding of hollowing from a lucid perspective. The player infers, but doesn't *experience* someone describing it.
- Less direct confrontation with the brother's state.
