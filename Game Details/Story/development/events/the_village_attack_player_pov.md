# The Village Attack: Player's Perspective

## The Moment Before

The player and brother are at the forest edge, north of the village. They've been collecting wood for repair work—the fallen trees from the winter storms are finally dry enough to work with. The brother is showing the player how to judge which pieces are strong enough, how to see the grain and predict where they'll split.

It's ordinary work. The kind that fills days.

The brother is quieter than usual. The player has noticed this over the past weeks—since the parents had that conversation in the evening, the one the player wasn't supposed to hear but did. Something about resources. Something about the village needing to decide what comes next.

The player doesn't ask about it. They just work alongside the brother, stacking wood, and try not to think about the weight of things unspoken.

The sound comes from the direction of the village.

Not an alarm-bell. Something else. A crash. Then another. Then a kind of chaos-sound that the player has never heard before. Not words. Not organized noise. Just something breaking and something screaming underneath it.

The brother goes still.

The player has learned to read the brother's stillness. This is not the thoughtful kind. This is the kind that comes before movement.

"Come on," the brother says.

They don't run at first. They walk quickly, wood forgotten, moving back toward the smoke that's starting to rise above the village treeline. The brother is calculating something. The player can see it in the set of his shoulders.

The smoke gets thicker.

## The Return

The village edge is chaos.

There are shapes in the plaza—humanoid but not. The player has learned to read bodies. These don't have that quality. They move wrong. Jerky. Like something has hollowed them out and left only the skeleton of motion.

People are running. Some toward the forest. Some trying to fight with whatever is at hand. Someone is screaming. There's blood on the plaza stones.

The brother doesn't hesitate. He's already moving into the fray, pulling the player with him. The player doesn't have time to think. Doesn't have time to understand what they're looking at.

There's a shape moving toward a group of children near the workshop. The player's mind catches on the wrongness of it—that thing shouldn't be able to move that fast, shouldn't be able to *want* anything. But it's moving toward the children with single-minded purpose.

The brother is suddenly there, between the shape and the children. He hits it with something—a piece of wood, maybe, or just his body. The thing doesn't fall. It barely pauses. But the children run.

The player is frozen watching this. Watching something that almost looks human reach for their brother. Watching the brother reach back not to defend but to *push*—pushing the thing away, buying a second.

That's when another shape comes from the side. The player sees it before the brother does. Sees it coming at an angle.

"Watch—" the player starts to shout, but it's too late.

The shape hits the brother. The player watches their brother get thrown backward, away from the dangerous shape, tumbling toward the workshop wall. For a moment the player thinks this is it, this is the moment, the brother is—

The brother moves. Even as he's falling, he's moving. He comes up against the workshop wall and redirects that momentum, launching himself back toward the thing. He's faster than it is. Not stronger, maybe, but *faster*. His eyes are burning with something the player has never seen before.

He grabs the player by the arm.

"Now," he says. Not a question. A command.

They run. The player runs because there's no other option, the brother's grip is iron and his eyes have gone to someplace the player doesn't have words for.

They burst out of the village plaza, back toward the forest edge, and the brother is pushing them forward, forward, *forward*, and the sounds of chaos behind them are getting further away but not far enough.

## The Carnage

They don't stop running until they're deep in the trees. The player is breathing hard, shaking, and the brother is just breathing, still holding the player's arm like he needs to confirm they're still there.

When they turn back, the chaos has paused. The shapes have stopped moving. Or maybe they're satisfied. The player doesn't know. Everything is wrong now. Everything has broken in ways the player doesn't know how to fix.

"We have to go back," the player says.

The brother doesn't say anything. He's staring at the smoke rising from the village with eyes that have gone flat. Something in his face has changed. The player can see him processing something, deciding something.

"Come on," he says finally. Not to the player. To himself.

They move back toward the village more carefully this time. The shapes are still there, in the plaza, but they're not attacking anymore. They're just standing. Waiting for something. Or maybe they've forgotten how to stop.

The player and brother skirt the edges, moving between the buildings.

That's when they find Kael.

Kael is in the street near the baker's workshop. Just... there. The player understands immediately that Kael is dead, but their mind won't *accept* it because Kael is still in one piece and death doesn't usually look like that. Death should be simpler.

The player walks past Kael without stopping. Can't stop. If they stop, if they look too long, something will break inside them that never repairs.

Kael's river stone is still in their pocket.

A few paces further: their father.

Thorne is at the edge of the plaza, between the buildings. He's clearly been fighting—there are overturned tools, scattered materials. He's been defending the workshop. Defending *something*. But the defending didn't work, and now he's just still.

The player looks at their father's face and understands that Thorne will never see the buildings he created again. Will never say "That's good work." Will never validate anything ever again.

The player feels the moment their chest becomes a weight they'll carry forever.

The brother is already moving again. His face is unreadable. He's checking each building methodically, moving with purpose. Looking for something.

That's when they hear her.

"... if anyone can find water, the children need water..."

It's Elsa's voice, coming from the forest edge. The player's heart does something complicated—jumps and breaks at the same time.

They move toward the sound, and they find Elsa organizing people at the forest boundary. She's bleeding. Her arm is torn and her movements are stiff with pain, but she's moving *people*. Moving them toward safety. Her face is calm in a way that terrifies the player—that calmness is Elsa making practical decisions while the world ends.

She sees the player and the brother and something in her expression breaks.

"Thorne?" she asks. Not even a question. A hope she's already giving up on.

The player can't say it. Can't make the words happen.

The brother doesn't say anything at all. He just looks at Elsa with eyes that have gone somewhere distant.

Elsa nods. Like she knew. Like she's been knowing this the whole time.

She turns back to the people she's organizing. Her movements are faster now. More urgent.

"Everyone to the deep forest," she says, and there's something in her voice that makes people listen. "Move. Now."

## The Mother

It takes a moment for the player to understand what they're seeing.

Elsa is moving people deeper into the forest, toward the safe camp area. She's limping. Her arm is badly wounded—the player can see now that it's not just torn, it's deeply cut. Blood is running down her hand and dripping to the forest floor.

"Mom," the player says, because they have to say something, have to anchor themselves to the fact that at least this one person—

"I'm fine," Elsa says, but her voice is thin. She's organizing supplies. Checking on injuries. Doing the work that needs doing.

The brother hasn't moved. He's still staring at nothing.

"Brother," Elsa says to him, and it's the most gentle thing the player has heard her say. "I need you to help the player. Help them process this. I need to keep moving people."

The brother nods, but it's not clear if he's heard her.

The evacuation continues through the evening. Elsa pushes through it all, moving people, checking on the injured, organizing the basic structure of survival. By the time the camp is established—scattered through the deep forest, hidden from the village—she's moving slower. Her wound has started bleeding more heavily. Her voice has gotten quieter.

By night, Kess, the healer's apprentice, is forcing Elsa to sit while she tends to the wound. The player watches their mother let someone else take care of her for the first time they can remember.

Elsa closes her eyes. When she opens them again, she looks directly at the player.

"Your father was brave," she says quietly. "He was protecting people when it happened. That matters."

The player nods because they don't know what else to do.

"And you're here," Elsa says. "You survived. That matters too."

The player doesn't feel like it matters. The player feels hollow.

## The Brother's Absence

By the morning of the second day, it becomes clear: the brother is gone.

He was there in the camp the previous night. The player remembers seeing him staring at the fire, not eating, not responding to anyone. But at some point in the darkness, he left.

Someone reports seeing him following the tracks of the shapes as they left the village. Not fleeing. Following. Deliberately moving in the direction the hollowed creatures went.

The player realizes that their brother has chosen to follow something the village doesn't even know how to name.

Elsa sits with the information with the same quiet composure she's brought to everything else. The wound in her arm is wrapped. She's pale from blood loss but moving through the practical necessities of camp organization. When someone asks where the brother went, she just says, "He's doing what he needs to do."

The player sits at the camp's edge that night, holding Kael's river stone. Kael who is dead. Thorne who is dead. Elsa who is alive and bleeding but alive. And the brother who is gone.

Not dead. Just... gone.

The player holds the stone and tries to understand what this means. That they can survive while others don't. That they can hold on to things while the person meant to protect them is chosen for something else.

The player doesn't know yet that the brother is following something toward understanding. That the brother is chasing the question the village doesn't even know it needs to ask.

All the player knows is that they're sixteen winters old and they've lost Kael and both parents in the same day, and they're still breathing, still holding a stone that thinks about them, still here.

The player holds the stone and begins the long work of becoming someone who can exist in a world that has this much absence in it.
