# The Brother's Departure — The Inciting Incident Scene

## Scene Context

**When:** Two days after the village attack. The immediate chaos has settled into numbness. Bodies have been laid to rest. Makeshift repairs are starting on damaged structures. The village is in shock — functioning on momentum, not emotion.

**Where:** The edge of the village, where the forest begins to the north. The brother has packed a small bag. He stands at the threshold between the community that has become unbearable and the wider world where he might find purpose.

**Who:** The player and the brother. Alone. Everyone else is occupied with immediate recovery work.

**Canonical anchors:**
- Brother cannot stay (village is a wound)
- He wants player to come (real emotional weight, not manipulation)
- He won't wait (he will leave with or without them)
- This creates the choice (attack creates conditions; departure creates fork)

---

## The Minimal Spine

**What must be true:**
1. The brother has decided to leave. This decision is not momentary — it's set.
2. He is asking the player to come. The ask is real and desperate.
3. The player hesitates (because the village needs them, because fear, because grief is heavy).
4. The brother waits for a moment, hoping the player will choose him.
5. The player's hesitation is read as refusal.
6. The brother leaves. Not angrily. Not spitefully. But finally.
7. The player is left alone at the threshold — having implicitly chosen the village by not choosing him.

---

## The Scene: Movement and Dialogue

### Opening: The Brother's Request

The player finds the brother standing at the village's northern edge, where the plaza gives way to forest. He's looking north, away from the village. His shoulders are set in a way the player recognizes — decision made, not reconsidering.

He doesn't turn around when the player approaches. He knows they're there.

```
BROTHER: "I can't stay here."

PLAYER: [reaction varies — can acknowledge this, ask why, express concern]

BROTHER: "Everything I look at is where they died. Thorne was standing there 
[gesture to workshop entrance]. Elsa was in the healing house 
[gesture toward west]. Kael...
[long pause]
I see their deaths more clearly than I see anything else."
```

This is not performative grief. It's clear observation of a fact he cannot live with.

```
BROTHER: "I can feel the shapes of what happened in every space. I walk into 
the plaza and I remember where I grabbed you and ran. I can't rebuild 
something when the only thing I see is what broke."

PLAYER: [response]

BROTHER: "Come with me."
```

The request comes matter-of-factly. Not desperate yet. Just stated as a logical conclusion.

### Inflection Point 1: The Real Ask

The player probably expresses hesitation (the village needs help, there's work, people are hurt). The brother hears this as refusal disguised as responsibility.

```
BROTHER: "The village will survive. These people know how to survive — 
they've done it for eighty years. But they won't know how to be 
whole again if the thing that attacked them can happen again."

PLAYER: [likely: we don't know what attacked us / we don't know where it came from]

BROTHER: "Exactly. We don't know. And I can't stay here not knowing. 
I can't wake up every morning in the place where grief lives and 
pretend to rebuild while something out there waits to attack again."

[pause]

BROTHER: "We lost the same people. Don't you want to make sure 
this never happens again?"
```

This is the emotional pivot. The brother isn't asking the player to abandon the village. He's asking them to fight for it in the only way he knows how — by pursuing the threat, by getting stronger, by making sure no one else dies like this.

It sounds noble. It IS noble. But it's also a need for control expressed as protectiveness, a grief expressed as purpose.

### Inflection Point 2: The Player's Hesitation

The player hesitates. Not because they don't understand the brother's pain — they share it. But because:
- The village is home and it's broken and leaving feels like abandonment
- The people still here need practical help (healing, repairs, organizing)
- The unknown is terrifying in a different way than staying is
- They're not sure who they are without the village as anchor
- Grief is heavy and they're not sure they can move it, let alone carry it while traveling

The brother watches this hesitation. He's looking for a yes. What he sees is the shape of refusal, even if the player hasn't said the word.

```
BROTHER: "I'm not asking you to abandon them. I'm asking you to 
come with me. To find what did this. To understand it. To make sure 
they're safe."

PLAYER: [response — can be unclear, conflicted, asking for time]

BROTHER: "I can't wait."
```

This is the moment. Not angry. Just final.

```
BROTHER: "If I stay here, I will hollow. I can feel it — the way grief 
wants to carve me out from inside. The only thing stopping it is the 
need to move, to act, to understand. If I stop moving, I stop."

[He turns to look at the player directly.]

BROTHER: "I have to go. If you're coming, you need to come now. 
If you're not... then I'll go alone."
```

### Closing: The Departure

The player has a moment to respond. But the brother's packed bag is at his feet. He's ready. His readiness is its own kind of answer.

If the player says yes: they walk into the forest with the brother. Scene transitions to the road, to the unknown. They begin the Strength/Power path or Strength/Knowledge path.

If the player hesitates or says no: 

```
BROTHER: "Okay."

[He picks up his bag. He doesn't hug the player. He doesn't say goodbye.]

BROTHER: "Rebuild the village. Learn its lessons. Stay here and 
make sure people know how to defend themselves."

[He pauses at the threshold.]

BROTHER: "But know that I'm out there carrying this alone because 
you chose to stay. And that grief, that loneliness — it's going to 
change me in ways you won't understand. When I come back, 
if I come back, I might not be the person you remember."

[He looks back one final time.]

BROTHER: "I hope you understand why I had to go."

[And he walks into the forest. He doesn't run. He doesn't look back again.]
```

If the player follows: they are in the story together. Their fates entangle. The brother has the player as a counterweight to his spiraling (or he drags them down with him).

If the player stays: the brother is alone. His hollowing has no counterbalance. The player is in the village with Kess, Thane, Kai, Senna — the community needs them, but the weight of watching the brother leave changes everything. They chose community. Now they have to live with what that means.

---

## The Brother's Voice and Presence

**Before he turns around:** Stillness. Economy of motion. This person has made a decision and is at peace with it.

**When he turns:** Clear eyes. Grief is in those eyes, but so is purpose. He's not confused. He knows this is necessary.

**His words:** Simple. Direct. Not lyrical or flowery. He states facts and emotional truths without apologizing for them.

**His body language:** Open (he's not hiding) but braced (ready for movement, ready to leave with or without the player).

**What he doesn't do:**
- He doesn't cry. Grief has moved past tears.
- He doesn't beg. He asks once, clearly.
- He doesn't shame. He states what he needs and accepts the player's choice.
- He doesn't promise to come back. He doesn't promise anything except that he's leaving.

---

## The Player's Choice (Not as Menu, but as Moment)

This is not "Choice A (Follow) or Choice B (Stay)." This is a **felt decision**.

The player experiences:
- The weight of grief they share with the brother
- The pull toward action (he's right, they were attacked, there should be answers)
- The pull toward the village (it needs them, abandoning it feels like betrayal)
- The fear of the unknown
- The fatigue of staying when everything hurts

They have **seconds** to speak. If they don't clearly commit to following, the brother interprets silence as no and leaves.

This is how the four paths emerge naturally, not mechanically:
- **Player says yes + brother focuses on power:** Strength/Power path
- **Player says yes + brother focuses on knowledge:** Strength/Knowledge path
- **Player says no:** Community path (by default, they're here with the village)
- **Community path + later abandonment:** Wandering path (they leave the village later after reconstruction)

---

## Thematic Weight

This scene is the TRUE inciting incident because:

1. **The attack is trauma. The departure is choice.** The village attack happens TO the player. The brother's departure forces the player to choose. Trauma is something that happens; choice is something you do.

2. **It sets up every path conflict.** The brother represents action, movement, purpose. The village represents rootedness, rebuilding, community. The player's answer to "Do I follow my brother or rebuild my home?" determines everything that comes after.

3. **It plants the brother's hollowing.** His need to move, to act, to ensure this never happens again — that's the mechanism. The player choosing to stay or go determines whether the brother hollows alone or has a counterweight.

4. **It makes every ending hit.** 
   - Community ending: The player stayed, so the brother hollowed alone. Now they have to confront what he became.
   - Wandering ending: The player followed, then abandoned him. Now they're searching for him in a world he's disappeared into.
   - Consolation ending: The player followed and stayed. Together they found a balance point.
   - Corruption ending: The player followed and they spiraled together. Now they're something else.

## Refactor Notes (for audit heartbeat)

- [ASSUMED] The departure happens 2 days after the attack. Canon doesn't specify timing, but 48 hours allows for immediate shock to settle into grief. Could be revised if Noble prefers different timeline.
- [ASSUMED] The brother has already made his decision before this scene. He's not wavering or reconsidering. He's at the threshold making a final offer. This interpretation emphasizes the player's choice over the brother's.
- [VERIFY] Does the brother's dialogue contradict his character? (No—he's stated as competent, warm before attack, then consumed by grief expressed as determination. This scene shows that turn.)
- [OPPORTUNITY] The brother's final line ("I might not be the person you remember") foreshadows his hollowing arc. This could be expanded or repeated in mirror form when he returns.
- [ISSUE] Does the brother's "I can feel the shapes of grief" language fit the world? This is poetic, but the brother is not stated as poetic. Consider making this more concrete/practical while preserving the emotional weight.
