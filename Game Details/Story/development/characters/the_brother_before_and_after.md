# The Brother: Before and After

## Name

**SAEL** (pronounced "sale")

A name that means something like "grief-bearer" in the old language, though by post-collapse times nobody remembers that. It's just a name. It sounds solid. Real. It can be said gently or with force, and both feel true.

## Before the Attack: The Reliable One

Sael is three years older than the player. That gap is important. It's just enough to make him not-quite-peer and not-quite-parent. He's old enough to have real competence (Thorne is teaching him everything about building). Young enough to find joy in things instead of just duty.

The player's relationship with Sael is fundamentally rooted in **trust**. Not the kind that has to be earned through demonstration. The kind that's just... there. Sael is someone who keeps his word. Someone who notices when the player needs help and helps without making a thing of it. Someone who makes the player feel *protected*, not controlled.

Before the attack, Sael is:

- **Quiet-confident.** Not showy about what he knows. Just does things well and moves on.
- **Warm with a backbone.** Genuinely cares about people. Also has clear opinions and isn't apologetic about them.
- **Proud of his role.** Works with Thorne on building. Practices magic with Elsa (lesser strength than the parents, but steady). Helps Mira with documentation sometimes. He's integrated into the village's functional structure and takes that seriously.
- **Protective.** The player realizes, years later, that Sael was always watching out for them. Not hovering. Just... present. Available.
- **Someone the player wants to be like.** Not because Sael demands it, but because Sael *is* something worth being.

### Key Pre-Attack Moment: The Carving

A few weeks before the attack, Sael is working on something. A carving. Not finished yet. The player asks what it is.

"Wait and see," Sael says. He's concentrating, carefully shaping something from wood.

The player forgets about it.

Two weeks later, Sael presents it: a stone. Not an actual stone—carved wood, shaped perfectly to look like one. Smooth. Round. Realistic enough that the player picks it up expecting weight and gets surprise at how light it is.

"It's for you," Sael says. "For when you need to remember that things can be shaped. Changed. Made into something different from what they started as."

The player doesn't entirely understand this at the time. It feels like a reference to something. But Sael doesn't explain further.

The player carries it in their pocket.

## The Attack: Sael's Transformation

During the attack, Sael does three things that matter:

1. **Moves with purpose.** While the player is still processing chaos, Sael is already calculating: how to protect the player, how to help others, where the threat is coming from.
2. **Saves the player.** Gets hit hard protecting them. Keeps moving anyway. Eyes burning with something the player will later recognize as rage so pure it looks like love.
3. **Leaves.** The player finds out later: Sael followed the attackers intentionally. Watched where they went. Followed.

In that moment—the attack—something breaks in Sael. Or rather, something locks into place. Some choice gets made that can't be unmade.

## The Departure: The True Inciting Incident

It's been two days since the attack. The village is in shocked camp-mode. Thorne is dead. Elsa is organizing people with mechanical precision, like she's following a checklist written into her bones. Kael is dead. Most people are wounded or in shock.

The player is sitting at the camp edge, holding Kael's stone and trying not to think about the way Thorne's hand looked when it stopped moving.

Sael finds them.

He sits down without preamble. There's dirt on his clothes and blood that might be his or might be something else. He looks like he hasn't slept or stopped moving.

"I'm leaving," he says. It's not a question or a discussion. It's information.

The player doesn't understand at first. "Tomorrow? To check—"

"Now," Sael says. "And I'm not coming back. Not until I understand what those things were and where they came from and how to make sure this doesn't happen again."

The player's chest does something complicated. "You can't just—the village needs—"

"The village needs people who can *stay*," Sael says, and his voice is gentle but absolute. "I can't. Every moment I'm here, every building I see that Father built, every place Mother taught people to heal—it's a wound. And I can't spend my energy staying steady when I could be using it to make sure there's never another attack."

"Then I'm coming with you," the player says. It feels obvious. Of course they're coming.

"No," Sael says.

"What?"

"You're not coming. You have to stay."

The player stands up. "You can't just leave me here. You just—you saved my life and now you're going to—"

"Sit down," Sael says, not unkindly. "Please."

The player sits because Sael's voice is still the one they trust. That's the awful part of this. The player trusts Sael, and Sael is asking them to accept being abandoned.

"Listen," Sael says. "I'm asking you to stay because you can. Because you're not carrying what I'm carrying. Because the village survived, and it needs you more than it needs me. And because—" He stops. Restarts. "Because I'm angry. I'm so angry that I can barely contain it. And that anger is going to push me into things that I can't come back from. I don't want you there when that happens."

"That's not your choice," the player says. "It's mine."

"It is my choice," Sael says. "And I'm using it to tell you: stay. Help Mother rebuild. Help Senna figure out how to lead a village that's broken. Figure out what you actually want instead of what I want."

The player tries a different angle: "How long are you going to be gone?"

Sael doesn't answer immediately. When he does, his voice is different. Smaller.

"I don't know. Maybe I'll come back. Maybe I'll find what I'm looking for and understand that coming back is possible. Or maybe I'll—" He stops. "Just. Don't expect me. Don't wait for me. That's not fair to you."

"You can't ask that," the player says.

"I know," Sael says. "I'm asking it anyway."

He stands up. It's clear this conversation is finished.

The player wants to hold onto him. Wants to grab his arm and refuse to let go. But Sael would leave anyway, and the player knows that. So instead, the player just sits there, holding Kael's stone, and watches their brother walk away into the darkness.

Sael pauses at the edge of the firelight.

"The carving I made you," he says. "Remember what I said? Things can be shaped. Changed. Made into something different. You're going to be made into something different from what you started as. But it will still be you. And that's going to be enough."

He leaves.

The player sits alone, holding two stones now: Kael's from the creek, and the memory of Sael's carving. Two different kinds of stones. Two different kinds of loss.

## The Aftermath: Sael's Arc Begins

From this moment on, Sael's hollowing arc is set in motion.

He doesn't deliberately drain himself (not yet). He moves into mana-depleted regions because that's where the attackers came from. He pushes himself harder because pushing is what keeps the grief from consuming him. Every time he casts magic in a drained area, he takes a tiny amount of himself. Carves away at his own reserves. Doesn't wait for the world to recover because waiting feels like surrender.

The player won't see him again for the rest of Act 1. But on active paths, they'll encounter evidence of him: Places he's been. People he's helped (and left). Settlements he passed through. Always moving. Always pushing forward.

And slowly, almost invisibly at first, Sael becomes something other than what he was. Not evil. Never evil. Just hollowed. Grief and rage and love all carved out of him, leaving space for something else to move in.

## Four Possible Outcomes (Echo of Canon)

1. **Community ending:** Sael spirals alone. The player stayed, rebuilt, learned restraint. They meet again with Sael far gone—the most dangerous because his motives were never simple. A choice has to be made.
2. **Wandering ending:** Sael is never seen again. He's either far gone into hollowing or disappeared entirely. The player may never know.
3. **Consolation ending (Knowledge-dominant):** At the edge but not over. The player's consistent pursuit (knowledge path) and presence in Sael's life becomes the counterweight. Recovery begins—not instant, not clean, but possible.
4. **Corruption ending (Power-dominant):** Both player and Sael deeply hollowed. They reunite as mirrors of each other. Together, they attack the mentor researcher to steal dangerous findings. They become what attacked their village.
