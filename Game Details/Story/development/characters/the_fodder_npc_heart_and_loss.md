# The Fodder NPC — Heart and Loss

## Character Identity

**Name:** MERYL (pronounced "MARE-ul")

A person in their late thirties. Sturdy build, the kind of body that comes from practical work—mending, carrying, building. Clear eyes, usually watchful but currently a little distant. They wear the clothing of someone from a working settlement: practical fabrics, well-mended, one good coat. Around their neck, a braided cord with a smooth stone on it—a talisman, or a memory, or both.

Meryl is from the settlement two days' journey from the cave. They were a healer's assistant and a community repair person—someone who fixed the small things that hold a settlement together. Quiet competence. Not flashy, but essential.

---

## The Spine: From Present to Loss

### Before the Disappearances

Meryl lived with their spouse, **CASS**. Together for eight years. Not remarkable, not dramatic—just solid. They shared a dwelling on the settlement's edge, kept a small garden, had routines that balanced work and rest.

Cass was a fisher and a gatherer. They knew the land around the settlement like someone knows their own body: every path, every seasonal resource, every shelter. When the settlement needed food, Cass went out and found it. They were good at returning with what was needed.

Meryl wasn't the romantic type, but they loved Cass with the kind of love that shows in small things: mending Cass's work clothes twice, making sure there was tea ready when Cass came home tired, keeping Cass's kit organized and ready for the next expedition.

### The Disappearances Begin

Three months ago, Cass went out to gather from a northern valley—a regular route, familiar, safe. They were supposed to return in two days. They didn't.

Meryl waited. Searched. Found nothing. No body, no gear, no trace. Cass had simply vanished. The settlement offered sympathies. Over time, offered less. Meryl searched alone for weeks. Nothing.

Then others started disappearing. The pattern became apparent: people who went to certain areas, particularly toward the cave system, didn't come back. The settlement grew afraid. They stopped sending people out. They stopped searching.

Meryl didn't stop searching.

### The Turning Point

When the party (player, brother, researcher) arrived asking about the disappearances, Meryl saw an opportunity. These people were going into the cave. These people had power that Meryl didn't. If Meryl went with them, they might find Cass. Or find what happened to Cass. Or find answers.

The party tried to talk Meryl out of coming. They weren't strong enough. They couldn't help in combat. They would be a liability. Meryl's response was simple: "My spouse is in there. I'm going."

They weren't reckless. They weren't trying to be a hero. They were a person who loved someone and needed to know what happened to them. That need overrode all other calculus.

## The Campfire Scene

This is the core of Meryl's development and the linchpin of their emotional impact. It needs to be written at full scene-level resolution.

### Setup

The party reaches the cave entrance late in the day. The surroundings are unsettling: barren, quiet, no animal sounds, minimal vegetation. The land feels wrong in a way that's hard to articulate. The researcher notes the mana absence. The brother doesn't care—he's ready to go in. The player makes a call about pacing.

Meryl is quiet. They're looking at the cave mouth, trying to see into the darkness, trying to sense if Cass is in there. They know they're not. They know the odds. But they need to look anyway.

The party decides to rest before entering. Too dangerous in the dark. They make camp just inside the cave mouth or just outside—some protected location. Fires are built. Watches are set.

Around the fire, as the evening deepens:

### The Conversation

**The Researcher** (curious, trying to distract from the weight of what's coming): "You've traveled this region for a while, Meryl? Before the disappearances started?"

**Meryl:** "Since I was small. Cass went out more than I did—the gathering routes, the hunting paths. I stayed in the settlement mostly. Repairs, healings, things that needed doing. But Cass would come back and tell me about places. I knew them secondhand."

**The Brother:** (direct, practical) "How long was Cass missing?"

**Meryl:** "Three months. Before the others started disappearing, we still thought maybe... lost, injured, unable to find their way back. After the pattern started, after more people vanished the same way, I knew. Something took them. Something lives out here."

A pause. The fire crackles.

**Meryl (continuing, voice steady but carrying weight):** "Cass wasn't supposed to be out there that day, actually. I'd asked them to stay. We were going to fix the storage shed together. It's a small thing, but I remember thinking I wanted an afternoon where we just worked on something simple, together. Cass said yes, but then Merrick came by asking for gatherers. New routes needed exploring, new food sources. The settlement needed it. Cass said they'd go for just that one day, they'd come back."

**Meryl:** "I said okay. I regretted it before they even left. Not because I knew something would happen. I just... knew them. Knew that sometimes Cass couldn't say no to what the settlement needed. I'd have let them go either way, but I did say 'come back for dinner.' Like it was something we could guarantee."

The Player watches Meryl. This is a person talking about someone they loved and lost, and the way they do it—with clarity and specificity and the kind of detail that comes from missing someone acutely—is breaking something open.

**The Researcher:** "What were they like? Cass?"

**Meryl:** (allowing themselves to smile slightly, into the fire) "Patient. Easier in the world than I was. They could sit with people and make them feel less afraid. Good at it without trying. They knew plants—not magically, just... knew them. Could look at a landscape and see what it wanted to give. They were a good person."

**Meryl:** "The stone I wear was theirs. A smooth river stone. They found it on a journey and gave it to me the day we moved into our dwelling together. Said it was smooth from the water wearing at it, and that good things can be made beautiful by patience. Cass was like that too—made things beautiful just by being patient with them."

**The Brother:** (unexpectedly quiet) "Do you think they suffered?"

**Meryl:** (a long pause, looking at the fire) "I don't know. I hope not. The missing people... nobody found their bodies. No signs of struggle. Whatever happened, it was fast. I tell myself that's better. I tell myself Cass didn't hurt. But I don't know. That's the worst part—not knowing."

The fire settles. The conversation ends there. No resolution, no comfort that works. Just the acknowledgment of loss and the weight of uncertainty.

### Reactions to the Conversation

**The Player:** Should be feeling the weight now. This is a person who loved someone and lost them, and that loss is about to get worse. The player is about to watch Meryl die trying to find them.

**The Researcher:** Should be intellectually processing while emotionally affected. This encounter—the mana absence, the disappearances, the Skelemancer forming from death—it's all connecting. But seeing Meryl's grief makes it personal in a way that data doesn't.

**The Brother:** Should reveal something about where he is on his own trajectory. Is he affected by Meryl's grief? Does he recognize something in it? Does it make him think about the player, about who he's leaving behind? His reaction here is diagnostic. A player paying close attention should read whether the brother is still emotionally accessible or if he's slipping further into the hollowing.

## The Death Scene

Later that night (the party sleeps, watches rotate), the bone-pull happens.

**The Shake:** It starts as a subtle vibration—internal, bone-deep. Everyone feels it at the same moment. The Skelemancer, far below in the cave, reaching upward, calling to every skeleton in its domain.

**The Resistance:** The party resists. Soul-level checks, mechanical and invisible to the player—but the narrative shows each person fighting it:
- The Player: Feels the pull, resists through willpower
- The Brother: Barely notices—his focus is unbreakable
- The Researcher: Struggles visibly, understanding what this means, resisting through sheer intellect
- Meryl: Can't resist. Has nothing to anchor them.

**The Moment:** As the shaking intensifies, Meryl realizes they're losing. Their body is moving against their will. They look at the party—not in panic, but in recognition. "It has me," they say, or perhaps don't say anything at all.

Then the red burst, the skeleton tearing free, the wet crunch of a body coming undone. It happens fast. Meryl collapses—what's left, which isn't much. The skeleton sprite tears across the ground and into the darkness of the cave.

**The Silence After:** The party stands or sits in shock. The researcher might understand it first—what just happened, what mechanism caused it. The brother might feel rage, or clarity, or hollowness. The player processes loss.

And Meryl is dead. The person they just sat around a fire with, the person who talked about their spouse with such care and specificity, is gone.

### What Remains

Meryl's body is still there—or what remains of it. The smooth stone is there, maybe loose in the ashes of the campfire, or still around where their neck was. The player can keep it, if they want. It becomes a memorial object, like the stone that Kael gave the player earlier.

Later (in Act 3 or beyond), if the player returns to the settlement, they can return the stone to Meryl's dwelling. A simple gesture that the settlement understands: "I found what happened to them. I couldn't save them, but I found out."

## Voice and Personality

**Meryl's Speech Patterns:**
- Direct, practical, no flowery language
- Uses Cass's name often—not avoiding the grief, acknowledging it
- Asks honest questions, willing to sit with uncertainty
- Doesn't explain or defend their choice to come—it was the only choice
- Speaks slowly sometimes, taking time to find the right words
- Isn't trying to be brave or heroic

**Example Dialogue:**
> "I'm not asking to fight. I'm asking to come. My spouse is out there and I need to know what happened. You can tell me I'm a liability. You're probably right. But I'm going either way. You might as well let me go with help."

> "Cass would have liked you. They were good at seeing people, seeing what they needed. They'd have thought you were carrying something heavy, and they'd have asked about it in a way that made you want to answer."

> "I don't need comfort. I need to know. If Cass is gone, I need to know how, so I can stop imagining worse things."

## Structural Role

Meryl is emotionally crucial but mechanically minimal. They're the price of the quest. They're proof that the world is dangerous in new ways. They're a mirror to the player's own losses (parents, childhood friend, potentially the brother).

Their death teaches:
- Not everyone survives their loss
- Grief can make people brave in reckless ways
- The world doesn't care how much you love someone
- Some mysteries get answered, but the answers don't comfort
