# The Attacker's Origin Settlement

## Locked Endpoints

- A nearby settlement that failed. Ran out of resources.
- The people kept taking from themselves. Hollowed out.
- Simple desperation produced simple, bestial forms — the creatures that attacked the player's village.
- Finding this settlement later is optional environmental storytelling, not a major questline.

## Overview

A community identical in structure to the player's village. Fifteen families. A central plaza. Functional dwellings. But empty. Recently. Everything left behind as if the inhabitants walked away mid-life. Through environmental storytelling, the player reconstructs what happened: desperation, resource depletion, a choice to take from themselves rather than abandon their home, and the gradual hollowing that followed. The village is a closed book of cause and effect.

## Geography & Layout

The settlement sits in a highland valley, two days' journey southeast of the player's village. High elevation, stable water source (a spring), but a valley with limited trade routes and difficult escape routes—a trap disguised as shelter.

**Layout (approximate, mirrors player's village):**

```
                        SPRING (water source)
                             |
           [Cultivation      |    [Storage     [Workshop/
            Fields]          |     Houses]      Crafting]
                             |
                    CENTRAL PLAZA
                    (gathering space)
                             |
    [Family Dwelling] [Family Dwelling] [Family Dwelling]
           |                 |                  |
    [Family Dwelling] [Shelter/Common] [Family Dwelling]
           |         (meeting house)     |
    [Family Dwelling]         |    [Family Dwelling]
                    [Defenses - minimal]
                    (crude wooden walls)
```

**Key structural observation:** The defenses are minimal. Crude wooden walls. Nothing reinforced. Nothing built for conflict. This was a community, not a fortress.

## Environmental Storytelling — Scene Sequence

The player approaches and enters gradually. Each location reveals one layer of the truth.

### 1. THE APPROACH — The Absence of Life

**First encounter:** The player spots the settlement from a distance. No smoke. No sound. No one working the fields. The cultivation plots are overgrown but not wild—the plants are there, but they're being left alone.

**Close approach:** The crude wooden gates stand open. No guard. No recent repairs. The hinges show rust from months of exposure.

**First structure:** A workshop at the settlement's edge. Tools remain inside: hammers, chisels, rope, wood planks. Everything organized, clean, as if the owner stepped out for the day and never returned. A fire pit is cold. Food scraps are old, dried, starting to dust.

**Emotional texture:** This is not the devastation of a raid. This is not the chaos of panic. This is the quietness of deliberate departure. The absence feels *chosen*.

---

### 2. THE PLAZA — Where the Decision Happened

**First impression:** The central plaza is the heart of the settlement. Roughly circular, open ground. A well sits at the center. Around it, the community buildings: storage houses, a larger shelter (meeting house), family dwellings.

**The ground itself:** Deep scratch marks scar the earth. Long, repeated grooves—the kind made when people stand in one place for a long time, shifting their weight. Some grooves are so deep they've worn through grass into bare soil. Others are shallower. This was not a brief gathering. People stood here. For hours. Days. Deliberating.

**The well:** Bone dry. Not even mud at the bottom. Completely drained. Recent—the lining still shows dampness marks where water was, not so long ago.

**A pivot stone:** At the center of the plaza, where community decisions would have been made. Marks are carved into it—vertical tallies, grouped in fives. Rows and rows of them. A count. Four months' worth of tallies. Someone was counting days. Or casts. Or something. The tally ends abruptly, mid-row. Unfinished.

**Emotional texture:** The marks on the ground show hesitation, debate, weight. The scratches are desperate rather than angry. The tallies are obsessive. This community was keeping score of something, and then... stopped.

---

### 3. FAMILY DWELLINGS — Lives Left Behind

**A typical dwelling interior:**

- A sleeping area with blankets folded neatly on a raised sleeping platform
- A common room with a clay table, clay bowls, wooden spoons
- A small personal space with belongings

**What remains:**
- Worn clothing, stacked or folded
- A clay pot with remains of stew, long dried to a brown crust
- Carved toys (wooden animals, simple)
- A journal (see below)
- Personal items: a carved stone, a shell, a piece of worked leather
- On one wall, marks carved into the wood (more tallies? Or something else?)

**Emotional texture:** These are not abandoned possessions. They are *left possessions*. Someone chose to leave them. This level of order suggests that the owners either:
- Had time to organize before departure
- Did not want to carry physical weight
- Did not expect to return

**A child's drawing:** On bark, crude but clear. Stick figures, a sun, grass, a bird. Below the figures, marks that might be letters, or attempts at spelling. The date is carved nearby: three months ago.

---

### 4. STORAGE HOUSES — Evidence of Depletion

**First observation:** These buildings are destroyed from the inside. Not by violence. By desperation.

**Interior scene:**
- Shelving systems are pulled from walls or dismantled entirely
- Grain stores are scattered across the floor—husks, spoiled grain, dust
- Root vegetables are rotted in piles, their smell still faintly present despite months passing
- Clay jars are shattered, their insides scraped clean
- The floors show evidence of organized searching—things overturned, moved, investigated

**What's clear:** Someone checked everything. Multiple times. Looking for food. Finding less each time.

**An artifact:** In one corner, beneath fallen shelving, a leather journal is wedged. Partially protected from the weather, its pages are brittle but readable. (See below for content.)

---

### 5. THE MEETING HOUSE — The Final Gathering

**Structure:** Larger than the other buildings. Open interior. Firepit at the center. No roof—it burned or collapsed, and no one repaired it. The walls remain.

**What remains:**
- Charred wood in the firepit
- Scattered bones (from meals, long since cleaned)
- Marks on the walls: hand prints, many of them, at various heights (adults and children)
- Carved into the wood beam that once held the roof: a single symbol. Unknown origin. Not a known spell. Not a known language. Something someone attempted to carve into being, perhaps after experiencing a vision. It's unfinished—the carver stopped mid-stroke.

**Emotional texture:** This is where the community came to gather. The handprints suggest they placed their hands on the walls, perhaps in a moment of unity or ritual. The symbol suggests someone was trying to call out to the world—or to magic itself—and failed.

---

### 6. THE DEPARTURE SITE — The End

**Location:** On the path leading from the settlement toward the player's village (southeast).

**Evidence:**
- A campfire, cold but recent (several weeks old by the ash and char)
- Cloth scattered nearby: a cloak, a wrap, remnants of clothing
- No bodies. No violence. Just *left clothing*, as if people shed what made them human and kept walking.

**A marker:** A crude cairn of stacked stones. At its center, a single item: a child's shoe, made of worked leather. Small enough to fit a child of five or six years. Placed carefully, intentionally.

**Additional marker:** Beside the cairn, carved into a tree: marks. Many of them. And a single symbol—the same symbol from the meeting house wall, but this one is completed. Finished. It's carved deep, with force. Whoever carved it knew what they wanted to say.

**Emotional texture:** The clothing suggests transformation. The cairn and child's shoe suggest remembrance—someone in the group held onto their humanity long enough to mark what they were losing. The completed symbol suggests a final act of communication, or acceptance, or defiance. The path ahead is marked with footprints, heading toward the player's village.

## Key Artifact — The Journal

**Location:** Discovered in the storage house beneath collapsed shelving.

**Condition:** Pages are brittle. Some entries are faded or illegible. But the core narrative is clear.

**Content (selected entries, chronologically):**

> **Entry 1 — dated ~5 months ago:**
> "The spring is still full. Harvest was good this year. Thorne thinks next year we'll have surplus enough to trade. I hope he's right. A trader came through yesterday and spoke of other settlements withering. We're fortunate. We're careful. We share what we have."

> **Entry 2 — dated ~4.5 months ago:**
> "The mana here has been weak for weeks. Or it has always been weak, and I'm only now noticing. Kess tried to use the healing symbol again—the one she learned from her mother—and nothing happened. She just stood there, hands shaking, trying to summon something that wasn't there. We told her to rest. But everyone noticed."

> **Entry 3 — dated ~4 months ago:**
> "We held the gathering today. Thorne spoke about moving. Finding a valley with stronger mana, or one less dependent on it. But where? The southern valleys are occupied. The northern mountains are dangerous. Everyone has refugees now. There's nowhere empty. So we stay. We always stay."

> **Entry 4 — dated ~3.5 months ago:**
> "Kin suggested we try drawing from outside the normal channels. Use magic in ways that some of us warned against when we first started casting. She called it 'emergency casting.' She said the mana is there, somewhere in the world. We just have to reach harder. Reach deeper. Some agreed. Thorne argued against it. They didn't let him speak for long."

> **Entry 5 — dated ~3 months ago (the last clear entry):**
> "I cast again today even though there was nothing left to give. I felt something break inside. Not painful. Just... empty. Like something important went away and left a space. The space filled with hunger. Not for food. For something else. Something I can't name. I see the others changing. We're all taking more. We're all emptier. Kin says it's necessary. Kin says we'll come through the other side. I don't know what that means anymore."

**Final pages:** Increasingly illegible. Sentences fragment. Words repeat obsessively. The handwriting becomes erratic. The last visible line is: "I can't remember my daughter's face without it hurting."

## What the Player Understands

As the player walks through this settlement, the reconstruction is visceral and involuntary:

1. **They were people.** The child's drawing. The folded clothing. The tallies of days. These are not abstractions. These are specific humans who lived lives.

2. **They had a choice.** No one forced them to stay. But they did. The village was their home, their identity, their stake in the world. Leaving meant admitting that choice had failed.

3. **Desperation is simple.** They kept taking from themselves because they thought survival was possible. They kept taking because stopping would have meant accepting failure. They kept taking until there was nothing left to take from.

4. **Hollowing is not corruption.** These were not evil people who chose power. They were desperate people who chose to continue existing, even as the cost rose beyond their understanding. The simple, bestial creatures that attacked the player's village were not monsters—they were the final form of a community trying not to die.

5. **The player's brother is heading in the same direction.** This is the key revelation. The brother left the village. He's pursuing something—power, answers, escape. And he's drawing from himself to get there. The player now understands what that path leads to.

## Optional: Brother's Passage Through

**If the player is on an active path** (where the brother is still alive and accessible), the brother may have passed through this settlement recently. Evidence:

- A campfire at the settlement's edge, warm within the last few days
- A mark carved into a tree (the brother's personal mark—established elsewhere in development)
- A note, carved into the stone of the pivot marker: simple, just enough. Examples:
  - "I understand now."
  - "They were trying."
  - "I can see what I'm becoming."

**The brother's presence here is crucial:** He saw the same ruins. He understood the same truth. And he *kept going anyway*. This is not ignorance. This is knowing and choosing. The player realizes the brother's path is not a mistake—it's a deliberate choice made with full awareness of the cost.

**Emotional impact:** The brother is more dangerous than the player thought, not because he's corrupted, but because he's *conscious* of the corruption. He knows what he's doing.

## Discovery Mechanics

The settlement is discoverable organically on all paths, though the trigger varies:

- **Power path:** Tracking hollowed creatures, the trail leads back to origin
- **Knowledge path:** Research mentions a nearby failed settlement; mentor or companion researcher suggests investigation
- **Community path:** Refugees or traveling merchants mention where the attackers came from; community sends the player to understand the threat
- **Wandering path:** Simple exploration; the player stumbles upon it by following old roads or asking travelers

**No quest marker. No quest giver. No reward.** The discovery IS the reward. The understanding is the point.
