# Act 2 Turning Point

## Locked Endpoints

- Recontextualize the player's understanding of magic's true cost — the hollowing, the void, what fills it.
- Raise the stakes for the brother's trajectory specifically.
- Ensure enough understanding that further corruption is a deliberate choice, not an accident.
- Work as a convergence of accumulated understanding, not a single dramatic reveal.

## Shelved: Hallucination Quest

Player perceives allies as monstrous during a corruption crisis. Two resolution paths: accept defeat (corruption reduced) or kill allies (corruption deepens).

**Why shelved:** Conflicts with the mirror metaphor between player and brother. Creates a retroactive "could they have been saved?" problem. Undermines the informed-commitment model.

**Could return as:** A late-game optional encounter for very high corruption players. Not a cure — just terrible clarity.

## ACTIVE DIRECTION: Option 1 — The Origin Settlement Discovery

*Selected by Noble on 2026-02-10.*

### Concept

The player discovers the settlement where the village attackers came from. Not a dramatic quest. Not a major narrative event. Just... finding it. Walking through the ruins. Understanding, step by step, that these creatures were people. That desperation hollowed them out. That the thing that attacked the village was the end result of choices made under pressure.

The recontextualization happens not through exposition, but through environmental storytelling and inference.

### The Discovery

**Trigger:** Varies by path, but something natural prompts exploration toward the origin settlement:
- **Power path:** Tracking corrupted beings to understand their pattern
- **Knowledge path:** Research hints at a nearby failed settlement
- **Community path:** Refugees or travelers mention where the attackers came from
- **Wandering path:** The player simply wanders and finds it by accident

**The Settlement Itself:**

A community similar to the player's village. Maybe fifteen families. Same scale. Same structure. But *empty*.

The buildings are intact — no violence here. No signs of attack. Just... abandoned. Recently. Within the past few months.

**Environmental Storytelling (Beat by Beat):**

1. **The first structure:** A family dwelling. Inside, signs of everyday life interrupted. A pot of food gone moldy. Clothes folded on a bed, never worn. A child's drawing on bark (stick figures, a sun, simple joy). The drawing is dated three months ago.

2. **The central plaza:** Similar to the player's village. A gathering space. Here, something different. Scratch marks on the ground where people gathered repeatedly. Deep marks, as if people stood in one place for a long time, shifting their weight. Deliberating. Deciding something.

3. **The storage buildings:** Empty. Not just "eaten," but *thoroughly* ransacked. Shelves pulled down. The ground scattered with spoiled grain husks, rotted root vegetables. Someone was looking for food. Desperately. Found nothing.

4. **A personal discovery:** Maybe a journal. Maybe a series of tally marks on a cave wall (counting days?). Maybe a letter, half-written, abandoned. Something that hints at *what was happening*.

Example journal entry:
> "We've been drawing from our own stores for three weeks. The mana is gone from this valley. Thorne says we should move, find a new place. But where? Everyone is in the same situation. The southern valleys are already taken. We can't leave. We have to wait. We have to try. I cast again today even though there was nothing left to give. I felt something break inside. Not painful. Just... empty."

5. **The departure scene:** Signs that people left suddenly, but not in panic. Deliberately. Organized. Their tracks head toward the player's village. They took what little they had. They marched as a group.

6. **The final piece:** Maybe a body. Or maybe just clothes, abandoned on the path as if someone shed their humanity like clothing. Not gruesome — just *wrong*. The implication is clearer than any explicit description: these people hollowed themselves out so completely that what's left isn't human anymore.

### What the Player Understands

Gradually, without exposition:
- The attackers were people with a home, a community, a shared life
- They didn't choose violence because they were evil
- They chose it because they were *desperate*
- Desperation and hollowing are connected
- The brother is heading in the same direction — toward desperation, toward the same choices

### The Brother's Role (Optional — Can Be Incorporated)

**If on active path:** The player finds evidence that the brother passed through here recently. A campfire still warm. A mark carved in a tree (his mark). He saw what happened here too. He understood. And he kept going anyway.

**Emotional implication:** The brother saw the same evidence. He *knows* what hollowing does. And he's still pursuing it. The player realizes the brother isn't making this choice from ignorance — he's making it consciously.

This moment can carry the "brother does something scary" weight: he's not a victim of circumstance. He's choosing power despite knowing the cost.

### Strengths of This Option

- **Environmental storytelling.** No exposition dump. The player infers the truth from evidence.
- **Universal understanding.** Works on all paths. Every player discovers the same place and makes the same realization.
- **Thematic resonance.** The hollow village mirrors the hollow people. The structure and emptiness reinforce each other.
- **Sets up attacker_origin_settlement discovery as a quest outcome** — if the player pursues it, they can find the settlement and report back to the village.
- **Carries the "they were people" requirement.** The journal entries, the child's drawing, the abandoned clothing — these are *specific people*, not abstractions.

### Weaknesses

- Requires environmental design/development to land emotionally. Can't be rushed.
- Doesn't provide *personal* understanding of hollowing from a lucid perspective. The player infers, but doesn't *experience* someone describing it.
- Less direct confrontation with the brother's state.

## REFERENCE MATERIAL: Option 2 — The Lucid Witness

*NOT SELECTED — Option 1 chosen (2026-02-10). Kept as reference material for potential future use or late-game optional content.*

### Concept

The player encounters someone in the *process* of hollowing. Not fully transformed yet. Still coherent enough to talk, to explain what's happening to them. A mirror. A preview. A person caught between states who can articulate the experience from the inside.

This person becomes the player's only window into what hollowing *feels like*, what the void *means*, and what fills it when someone keeps taking from themselves.

### The Encounter

**Trigger:** Varies by path, but the player finds this person naturally:
- **Power path:** While hunting corrupted beings, the player stumbles on someone still partially human
- **Knowledge path:** A person seeking the researcher/mentor for help with what's happening to them
- **Community path:** A refugee arrives at the village seeking aid, reveals their condition
- **Wandering path:** Found resting in a liminal space (ruins, crossroads, threshold)

**The Person:**

**Name:** [TBD — call them "The Half-Hollowed" for now]

They look human but *wrong*. Not a monster yet. Just... incomplete. Their movements are too precise or too loose, as if they haven't quite reconciled their body with their intention. Their eyes are sharp in moments, then vacant. Speech is coherent but punctuated by long pauses where they seem to be listening to something the player can't hear.

They're dangerous. The player can sense this. But not immediately hostile. Not yet.

### The Conversation

**The person knows they're changing.** This is crucial. They're not delusional. They understand what's happening and they can explain it.

"I've been casting for weeks without stopping. When the mana ran out, I kept casting. I drew from myself. It was... convenient. I didn't feel desperate about it at the time. I just needed to keep going. The world was broken. I had to fix it."

(Long pause.)

"Something broke inside me. Not painfully. Just... a gap opened up. And something filled it. Something that wasn't me before. Or maybe it was always in me, and now it's all of me."

"The void doesn't hurt. That's the trick. It doesn't feel like loss. It feels like clarity. Like everything that was confusing and complicated has been burned away, and now there's only one thing that matters. One drive. One truth."

(Eyes focus suddenly, intensely.)

"For me, it's anger. Not the confused anger you feel in life — the anger at unfairness, at loss, at helplessness. This is pure anger. Refined. It's almost beautiful. If I let myself think about it, I can feel it spreading, taking everything else that was me and converting it into fuel."

"The worst part is I want it. I want to let it finish. Because the alternative is going back to being confused and scared and uncertain. And I can never be that again."

### What Makes This Dangerous

**This person is at a choice point.**

They could recover if they stopped casting, rested, let the world regenerate the mana around them, and slowly filled the void with intention instead of letting it fill itself. But this requires stopping. Waiting. Sitting with uncertainty.

Or they could keep going. Let the hollowing complete. Become something that doesn't have to think about it anymore.

**If on active paths with the brother present:** The brother might encourage completion. "Let it finish. You'll be stronger." Or the player might watch the brother recognize himself in this person and see, for the first time, that he might be heading down the same path.

If the player tries to help the half-hollowed person stop and rest:
- **It's almost impossible.** They can't bear the emptiness. It needs to be filled.
- **But it's possible.** If the player sits with them, offers grounding (anchors to identity, memory, relationship), they might choose to stop.
- **This costs something.** The player uses time and attention. The player might suffer damage (mana, health, emotional texture) from being near someone mid-hollowing.

### The Choice the Player Makes

This moment crystallizes the hollowing question: "What do you do when someone is carving themselves out and filling themselves with one dominant drive? Do you try to save them? Do you accept they're choosing this?"

If the player helps the half-hollowed person stop and rest, they survive. They don't fully recover, but they stop getting worse. They become a person shaped by what they've learned about themselves — dangerous, changed, but not corrupted.

If the player doesn't intervene or encourages completion, the person finishes hollowing. They become a corrupted being — potentially an ally (if hollowed with the same drive as the player), potentially a threat (if their drive conflicts).

**Either way, the player has learned the mechanism from the inside.**

### What the Player Understands

- Hollowing is a choice, even if it doesn't feel like one
- The void fills itself with whatever's loudest inside you
- Stopping is harder than continuing, but not impossible
- The brother — if present — recognizes himself in this person and you can see him considering whether to stop or keep going
- Corruption isn't sudden. It's gradual. It's a series of small choices that become a personality

### The Brother's Role (Incorporated)

**If on active path:** The brother is here too. He witnesses this person. And depending on his own trajectory and the player's choices:

- **If the player helps the half-hollowed person stop:** The brother sees that it's possible to choose differently. He might not choose differently (his stakes are higher, his losses are deeper), but he *knows* it's possible. This creates doubt in him, which is the beginning of change.

- **If the player doesn't help or lets them finish:** The brother sees his future. Sees what completing the hollowing looks like. He might double down or he might recoil. Either way, the moment has weight.

**The scene can carry the "brother does something scary" without requiring a separate confrontation.** His recognition of himself in the half-hollowed person, his silent consideration of whether to stop or keep going, his choice to keep going *despite* knowing the cost — that's the scary thing. Not a dramatic act. Just the moment of understanding what he's choosing to become.

### Strengths of This Option

- **First-person understanding.** The player hears from inside what hollowing feels like, not just infers it from ruins.
- **Active choice.** The player can intervene or not, creating agency.
- **Personal confrontation with the brother's state.** If the brother is present, this mirrors his trajectory explicitly.
- **Emotional resonance.** A person still partially human is more emotionally accessible than ruins or external observation.
- **Universal understanding.** Works on all paths. Every player encounters this person and makes their own choice about helping.

### Weaknesses

- Requires strong character work and dialogue to land emotionally.
- The person's explanation of hollowing might feel too expository if not carefully crafted.
- Less environmental storytelling. More dialogue-dependent.
- Could feel like a "teaching moment" if handled poorly (RND constraint: avoid this).
