# Structural Cohesion: One Game, Not Four

## The Shared Emotional Question

"What do you do with loss?"

Power says: "Prevent it." Knowledge says: "Understand it." Community says: "Rebuild from it." Wandering says: "Accept it."

The brother embodies the question in its rawest form. His fate is the measure of the player's answer. This question is present in every path, every act, every interaction. It's what makes the game feel unified regardless of which compass direction the player leans.

## NPC Design Principle

Each NPC's pitch is convincing from their perspective and genuinely flawed from the others'. No one is lying. Everyone is incomplete.

---

## Four Layers of Cohesion

### LAYER 1: WORLD EVENTS (Shared Reality)

The world operates on a timeline independent of the player's path. The same things happen out there — settlements fall, mana zones deplete, factions rise, the brother acts. What changes is which parts of those events the player witnesses firsthand, hears secondhand, or misses entirely.

**Example — a settlement falls to corruption:**
- The power player fights what came out of it.
- The researcher walks through it and understands why it fell.
- The farmer deals with refugees arriving at the village.
- The wanderer finds the ruins months later, overgrown and quiet.

Same event. Four angles. One world. None is "the real version." All are partial.

### LAYER 2: SHARED VERBS (Mechanical Identity)

Every path uses the same core mechanics: casting magic, exploring spaces, managing mana, interacting with NPCs, surviving encounters. The farmer fights wolves threatening crops with the same magic system the power player uses against corrupted beings. The wanderer navigates dangerous terrain with the same stamina and resource management. The context for using mechanics changes. The mechanics themselves don't.

### LAYER 3: THE BROTHER AS THROUGHLINE

No matter what path the player takes, the brother's arc is happening. The power player walks beside him. The researcher encounters him at the same sites. The farmer hears about him from travelers returning to the village. The wanderer finds traces of his passage in distant places. He's the one story thread every version of the game shares, experienced differently but always present. His fate is the emotional constant.

### LAYER 4: THE CROSSROADS (Shared Space)

No large hub town exists — that would contradict the lore of isolated, post-collapse communities and flatten the diversity of small villages with their own cultures and superstitions. Instead, a crossroads: a meeting place that emerged naturally from geography. A river crossing, a mountain pass, a junction of old roads. People don't live there permanently. It's a liminal space — a periodic bazaar or seasonal gathering point where different villages send people for trade and information.

The brother passes through because everyone does eventually. The farmer trades there. The researcher finds scholars passing through. The wanderer rests. Same place, different reasons, different NPCs foregrounded. It preserves the isolation of individual communities while giving the paths a natural point of intersection. Not a safe haven. Not governed. Just geography forcing people to overlap.

---

## Why Mixed-Path Players Don't Break It

A player who switches emphasis mid-game doesn't break the story — they shift their angle on the same unfolding reality. Because world events happen regardless, the player who samples multiple paths gets a broader but shallower picture. The player who commits deeply gets a narrower but richer one. Both are valid.
