# The Player (Protagonist)

## Locked Endpoints

- Born into the post-collapse world. Has grown up in the aftermath of the Fall, but magic is new — discovered only about a year ago. The player has had time to experiment with basic casting, but magic still feels fresh, uncertain, and not fully understood by anyone.
- Grew up in a humble village of about fifteen families. No currency, only cooperation.
- Parents killed during the coordinated village attack by hollowed creatures.
- Childhood friend killed in the same attack.
- Life saved by their older brother during the attack — a clear, visceral moment.
- Left in a damaged but living village after the brother departs.
- Not a chosen hero. Someone shaped by loss and forced into choice.

## Established Traits

- Willing to experiment, travel, and learn.
- The player's compass orientation is determined entirely by player choices — no predefined personality.

## Key Relationships

- **The Brother** — only surviving immediate family. Shared grief, shared loss.
- **The Village** — home, community, the place the attack happened.
- **The Four Path NPCs** — each offers a different response to loss.

## The Core Question

"What do you do with loss?" — the player's answer defines the game.

## Daily Life: Morning

The village wakes the way it always does. The player is maybe sixteen winters old, already tall enough that ducking through doorways is becoming a habit. The morning light comes soft through the window of the house their parents built — *Thorne's* house, people still call it, the carpenter's place, though by now it's been expanded twice and the interior walls are all their mother's designs.

The player wakes because they always wake at the same time. Not from an alarm — there are no alarms — but from the rhythm of the village. It's the time when someone starts ringing the bell at the central plaza, not loud, just a tapping. A reminder. The village is small enough that you know who does it on which day. Today it's Marcus, who tends the communal stores.

They dress in layers. The mornings are cool, even in what the older people call summer. The world is different now than it was before the Fall — cooler, stranger, wilder. But the village is stable. It has been for as long as the player has been alive.

Their mother is already awake. Elsa. She's in the back room — the healing room — organizing things. Bundles of herbs hang from the ceiling, tied with cord their father made. There are jars of things: honey infused with goldenroot, crushed willow bark, things the player's mother learned from her own mother and is still learning from the strange world itself.

"Morning," Elsa says without looking up. She's arranging fresh moss — gathered from the north forest, the old logging area. "Did you sleep?"

"Yeah," the player says. It's true, mostly. There were dreams, but not bad ones. Just strange. Dreams about the symbols Kael draws, the ones that glow faintly if you look at them in the right light.

"Your father wants you at the workshop today. We need the north wall reinforced before the rains."

The player nods. This is rhythm. Predictable. The village moves according to what needs to happen, and people do the work. Some people farm, some fish the river, some tend animals. The player's father builds. The player is learning to build.

Their mother doesn't look up from her work, but she reaches out without looking, and the player understands this is an invitation. They lean down, and she touches their forehead with her palm — something she's done since they were small. It's warm. Grounding. She smells like the healing room, like herbs and something else that's just her.

"Stay steady today," she says. Not a question.

The player knows this is about something. Not something bad, but something *off*. The adults have been different for the past few days. Quieter. Watching the roads more carefully. But when the player asked, Kael said it was probably nothing. Kael is curious about things like that — the small wrong feelings that maybe mean something, maybe don't.

## Daily Life: Midday

The workshop is half-built. Well, built — functional — but always being built. That's Thorne's philosophy. Things are never complete; they're always improving. The north wall is indeed leaning slightly. Not dangerous yet, but Thorne can see it before anyone else can.

The player works with their father, passing tools, holding things steady. Thorne doesn't talk much, but he explains what he's doing. Not to teach, exactly, but just... this is how he thinks out loud. He's saying the wall shifted because the earth beneath it is settling differently in the eastern corner, and if you don't account for that, the whole structure becomes unstable. So they're going to reinforce the foundation, and they're going to do it with enough strength that the building can shift later without failing.

"Anticipate," Thorne says, more to himself than the player. "Build for the thing that hasn't happened yet."

The player understands this about their father: he's thinking three years ahead, five years ahead. The village will grow. The rains will come harder. The world will keep changing. So everything he builds needs to be flexible and strong.

As they work, Kael appears at the edge of the clearing. Kael is always appearing — they move through the village like they're following paths no one else sees.

"Reinforcement?" Kael asks, approaching the workshop. They're the same age as the player, maybe a few months younger. Where the player is learning to be steady and grounded like Thorne, Kael is learning to be curious about anything.

"North wall," the player confirms.

"Can I watch?"

Kael settles on a piece of wood, knees drawn up. They pull out something from their pocket — a piece of smooth river stone, white and almost translucent. They've been obsessed with stones lately. Not for any practical reason. Just because they're interested in why things are shaped the way they are.

"Do you think the magic knows?" Kael asks suddenly, turning the stone over in their hands.

"Knows what?" Thorne asks, handing the player a beam to position.

"That we're using it. That the village is built on the mana lines. That we're doing something with the magic even when we're not casting spells." Kael's voice is wondering, not worried. "Like, consciousness, but not like ours. Magic is aware somehow, but aware of... what? Rules? Patterns?"

The player doesn't answer immediately. They position the beam while Thorne holds it steady, checking the angle. This is the thing about Kael — they ask things that don't have answers, and they seem genuinely curious about whether anyone *else* thinks they might.

"Magic doesn't think," Thorne says evenly. "It responds. We cast the symbols, it responds. That's the whole system."

"Yeah, but what if it's simpler than that?" Kael says. "What if it doesn't think, but it *does* — like, it flows in patterns because that's just what it is. Not because it's alive, but because being aware doesn't require thinking. Plants are aware, right? They know when the sun is there without thinking about it."

The player realizes they're smiling. This is Kael. Always pushing the questions deeper.

"Your stone is aware too?" the player asks, teasing gently.

"Maybe!" Kael says. "Maybe that's why I like it. It's been aware, very slowly, for thousands of years. Just sitting in the river, learning about water."

The work continues through the afternoon. The beam is set. Thorne shows the player how to reinforce the joints — this part is mostly practical, about making sure the wood doesn't split under pressure. By the time they're done, the light has shifted. It's the golden time, as Elsa calls it. Late afternoon, the sun low and long.

"Thank you," Thorne says to Kael. "For keeping us company."

It's a formal kind of thing to say, but Thorne means it. The village is small enough that solitude during work is unusual. People work together, even if it's just sitting and existing near each other.

The player walks Kael toward home as the light fades. They know that by tomorrow, something will be different about the world. Not worse, not better — just different. But tonight, walking through the village with Kael, everything is still the way it's been.

## The Thing About the Player

The player doesn't have a grand destiny. They've never been told they were special. They're not unusually brave or clever. They're just... there. Present. Learning to build like their father, learning to heal-think like their mother, learning to be curious like Kael.

When asked what they want, the player doesn't know yet. The village is stable. The work is clear. Magic is a system they're learning, not a calling.

But they have something: a capacity to notice. To see what Thorne sees — the small shift in foundations that will matter later. To understand what Elsa values — steadiness, presence, the quiet kind of care. To follow Kael into questions that don't resolve.

These will matter. But not yet. Not until everything breaks.
