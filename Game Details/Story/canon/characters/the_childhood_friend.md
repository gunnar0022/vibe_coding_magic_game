# The Childhood Friend

## Locked Endpoints

- Dies during the coordinated village attack by hollowed creatures.
- Must be a SPECIFIC PERSON — not narrative fuel, not an archetype.
- The player should remember something about them.

## Known Relationships

- **The Player** — childhood friend. The loss is personal and specific.

## Name & Role

**Kael** (the childhood friend) — No official role in the village. Just... someone who notices things. Collects strange plants. Draws on bark with charcoal. Asks questions about why the world works the way it does. The kind of person who makes you see familiar places differently because they're always looking for the *interesting* thing, not the *practical* one.

## Character Texture

Kael is restless. Not in an angry way — in a curious way. They explore the edges of the village and the forest beyond it. They bring back things: a smooth stone from the creek with an odd pattern, a bird's nest (carefully, after the birds left), a leaf they swear has never been seen before. They show them to the Player like treasures. "Look at this. Look at *this*. Do you see how the light moves through it?"

The Player and Kael grew up together like siblings, except Kael is not a sibling. Kael is the person who reminds the Player that the world is strange and worth wondering about. While the Player's parents taught them competence and contribution, while the brother learned excellence and pride, Kael teaches: *noticing is its own kind of work.*

Kael doesn't understand magic or much about the new world they're in. Kael sees the *mystery*. They're fascinated by it. They want to understand how it moves, where it comes from, why some places feel different from others.

There's a gentleness to Kael. They're not soft exactly, but they move through the world without the urgency that drives Thorne or the brother. They sit with things. They observe. They wait to understand before they act.

# THE CREEK SCENE — Kael and the Player

## The Scene That Shows Who They Are

**Setting:** A rocky outcrop just outside the village, overlooking the creek valley. The water moves fast here, tumbling over stones. The vegetation along the banks is thick — unnervingly so, the way everything grows now. Mid-afternoon. Warm.

**Context:** The village doesn't understand magic. Not really, this is a small farming community, not a center of learning. A few people have gotten a couple of symbols to work — enough to be useful, enough to be a little frightening. Nobody can explain why it works. Most people don't try. Some mutter about gods. Some think it's poison. The brother has been practicing obsessively, convinced it's a tool they need to survive. Their mother uses a simple water-cleansing symbol she picked up from a passing trader, and treats it the way she'd treat any new chore — carefully, without thinking too hard about it.

Kael doesn't cast. Kael watches.

---

**The Player** has slipped away from afternoon work to find Kael. They do this sometimes. Kael is always in the same spot, or nearby, sitting with that particular stillness that would look like laziness to anyone who didn't know them.

Kael is on the flat stone above the creek. When the Player arrives, Kael doesn't say anything. Just shifts over slightly, making room.

They sit together for a while. The creek sounds fill the silence. The vegetation along the banks sways in a rhythm that doesn't quite match the wind.

"Do you notice anything?" Kael asks. Not urgently. The way you'd ask someone if they could hear a distant sound.

The Player looks around. Creek. Rocks. Trees. The thick undergrowth that's been growing faster every year since before they were born.

"About what?"

"Just... here. Compared to other places."

The Player doesn't know what Kael means. Kael struggles with it for a moment, visibly searching for words that don't exist yet.

"Okay. You know the dry field past the north wall? The one where nothing grows right?"

"Yeah."

"How does it feel there?"

The Player shrugs. "Empty? I don't know."

"Right. And here?"

The Player pauses. Considers it honestly for the first time. Here feels... different. Not just prettier or greener. Something else. Something underneath that they've never tried to name because it's never occurred to them that it needed a name.

"It feels, fuller here. Doesn't it?" Kael asks

"I suppose it does? What are you getting at?" The player is still confused.

"I've been watching," Kael says. "Here, by the fast water, where everything grows thick — it's stronger. Up on the dry ridge, there's much less."

The Player stares at Kael. This is more words than Kael usually strings together.
"Much less what?" 

Kael shakes his head and continues on.
"The Harmon family was practicing that fire symbol for three days straight last month. The air around their house felt like the dry field afterwards. Then it came back after they stopped."

... "are you saying?"

"I think there's something here.. Everywhere. " Kael continues. "Not the magic people are casting. Something under that. Something they're using up when they cast. I think it comes from..." 

Kael gets a clever look in their eye. "Watch closely. - not on me, but what you can feel. "

Then, he closes his eyes in concentration, and slowly, he channels the magic to make a large bubble of water before him. Likely the most simple spell. 

This is when the player's environmental mana bar appears. When Kael casts, they notice it go down slightly. 

The player: "!!! I think I .. I felt it. "

Kael grins, releasing his concentration and lets the water lose it's shape and fall with a splash. He gestures vaguely at the creek, the trees, the thick undergrowth. "For some reason, these places seem to be more full."

They sit with that for a long moment. The creek keeps moving. The plants keep swaying in their not-quite-wind rhythm.

"I don't know how to describe it.. But it's kind of obvious after you notice, right?" Kael says. Quietly.
"I suppose.. " the Player admits.


Kael watches the Player's face and sees the recognition. They smile — small, relieved. The smile of someone who's been carrying something alone and finally has company.

"I knew you'd see it."

Before they head back to the village, Kael pulls out something wrapped in cloth. A small stone from the creek bed — smooth, pale, catching the light in a way that seems almost deliberate. The kind of thing that's everywhere and nowhere, unremarkable unless someone picked it out and held it up for you.

"Here," Kael says, pressing it into the Player's hand. "Found it today. Keep it."

The Player looks at it. It's just a stone.

"It was sitting right where the water moves fastest," Kael adds. "Right in the middle of all of it. I think it soaked some up."

Whether that's true doesn't matter. What matters is that Kael noticed a stone in a creek, thought it was beautiful, and wanted the Player to have it. That's who Kael is. That's everything about Kael, in one gesture.

They walk back to the village together. The mana bar stays.

## Design Notes

**The mana bar is Kael's gift.** The most important mechanical feedback system in the game — the warning that prevents hollowing, the signal that tells the player when to stop — was given to them by a person who dies shortly after. Every time the player glances at that bar for the rest of the game, it carries Kael's fingerprint. The mechanic is a memorial.

**Kael discovered the mana field through temperament, not intelligence.** They weren't studying. They were sitting still in a world where everyone else was busy surviving, and they noticed what nobody else noticed. This is consistent with the world — the discovery doesn't require sophistication. It requires presence. Kael had the one thing nobody else in the village could afford: patience and attention without agenda.

**The village's relationship to magic should feel primitive.** A few working symbols picked up from traders or found on old-world remnants. No theory. No framework. No real understanding. The brother drills because he thinks it's a weapon. Their mother uses one spell like a kitchen tool. Nobody is thinking about what magic *is*. They're thinking about what it *does*. Kael is the only person in the village asking the deeper question, and they don't have the vocabulary for it.

**The brother is absent from this scene.** This moment belongs to the Player and Kael alone. The brother is elsewhere — practicing, working, doing something he considers productive. He wouldn't sit on a rock watching water. He never received what Kael gave the Player, and that asymmetry matters when grief hits them both differently.

**The creek stone stays in the Player's inventory.** It does nothing mechanically. It sits there. The player scrolls past it every time they open their bag. Late in the game, the description could shift subtly based on the player's path — reflecting who they've become since the person who gave it to them died.

## Why This Moment Matters

1. **Establishes who Kael is** — not a role in the village hierarchy, but a distinct person with their own way of seeing the world. Curious, gentle, philosophical about magic.

2. **Shows why the friendship is real** — Kael offers something the Player's family doesn't. A way of being that's not driven by competence or mastery or contribution. A way that's simply... *interested*.

3. **Plants a seed for later** — Kael's theory that magic is aware, that it notices us, that there's a relationship there, becomes relevant later if the Player learns about hollowing and corruption. The thing that makes the magic aware of our need to take from ourselves.

4. **Gives the Player a specific memory to carry** — That stone. That moment. The thing Kael said about it thinking of them. When Kael dies, the Player has something concrete to remember them by.

## Death Endpoint (Not a Scene — Just a Note)

Kael dies during the coordinated assault. Possibly trying to protect something or someone, or simply caught in the path of the creatures. The specific moment is less important than the fact that this person — who existed outside the village's functional hierarchy, who taught the Player to wonder — is simply *gone*.

For the Player, the loss is different from losing the parents. The parents were structural — they taught survival and values. Kael taught joy and strangeness. A different kind of breaking.

## Overview

Kael's presence in the game is brief—they die in the prologue attack—but their impact is disproportionate because the moments they shared with the player establish trust and affection fast. These scenes show Kael as a specific person the player loved, not an abstraction.

## Memory 1: The Creek Discovery (Daytime Scene)

### Context
This is the scene from the previous development file, refined with more specific detail.

**Setting:** The rocky outcrop overlooking the creek valley, mid-afternoon. The place is visibly charged with ambient mana—water moves faster here, light has a quality to it, the air feels alive.

**What Happens:**
Kael is waiting when the player arrives (this is a regular meeting spot). They sit together in silence for a moment. Then Kael points:

"Look at the water. Watch how it moves."

The player watches. They've seen water their whole life, but Kael's attention makes them *see* it differently—not as a resource or a practical thing, but as something with its own motion and intention.

Kael speaks quietly, almost to themselves:

"I think it's listening to the magic. The magic moves through it and the water listens and moves with it. They're talking."

The player doesn't fully understand, but they recognize the feeling: Kael is noticing something true that the rest of the village hasn't caught yet.

**Kael pulls out the stone.** It's smooth, worn, with a pattern that catches light strangely.

"I found this today. It's been in the creek for years, maybe. The water and the magic wore this pattern into it. That's how long they've been talking."

Kael hands it to the player. It's warm from being in the sun.

"Keep it. When you don't understand something, hold it. It'll remind you that the world makes sense in ways we haven't learned yet."

**What the Player Carries Away:**
- The stone (physical memory object)
- The feeling of Kael's certainty about invisible things
- A moment of calm observation, distinct from the urgency that defines the rest of the player's family

## Memory 2: The Brother's Assessment (Evening Scene)

### Context
This scene shows Kael and the brother in the same space. Their dynamic is important because it will inform how the player interprets each of them.

**Setting:** The village plaza, late afternoon. The brother is doing some training or repair work (something active and purposeful). Kael is sitting nearby, watching. The player observes them together.

**What Happens:**

The brother notices Kael watching and pauses:

```
BROTHER: "You're always just... sitting. Don't you ever feel like you should be doing something?"

KAEL: "I am doing something. I'm learning how you move."

BROTHER: "Learning what?"

KAEL: "Why you move that way. You move like you're fighting something. Even when you're not fighting."
```

This is not unkind observation. Kael's tone is genuinely curious, not critical. But it lands on something true: the brother is always in motion, always directed, even when there's no threat.

The brother doesn't like this assessment much:

```
BROTHER: "The village needs people who do things. Not people who just watch."

KAEL: "The village needs both. You need to know what you're doing things *for*. That's my job."
```

Kael returns to watching. The brother returns to work, a little unsettled.

**What This Shows:**
- Kael sees clearly (the brother IS always fighting something)
- Kael respects the brother's drive without being driven the same way
- The brother respects Kael's observation, even when it's uncomfortable
- There's no hierarchy between them—they simply operate differently

**Later, Privately:**

The brother mentions this to the player:

```
BROTHER: "Kael's weird, you know that?"

PLAYER: "Yeah."

BROTHER: "But they're right. About me, I mean. I do move like that."

PLAYER: "Does that bother you?"

BROTHER: "No. It's just... Kael notices things other people don't. I don't know what I'd do if something happened to them."
```

This small moment is important: the brother cares about Kael, even if (or because) Kael sees through his intensity.

## Memory 3: The Naming Moment (Prologue, Before the Attack)

### Context
This scene happens during the normal, undamaged day before the attack. It shows Kael and the player at their most comfortable together.

**Setting:** Somewhere quiet—maybe near the healing house where Elsa is working, or at the workshop where Thorne is building. Kael and the player are sharing a task or just being near each other.

**What Happens:**

Kael asks a question that shows how they think:

```
KAEL: "Do you ever wonder what the stone is called? The one I gave you?"

PLAYER: "It's just a stone."

KAEL: "No, I mean what it calls itself. What it thinks its name is."

PLAYER: "Stones don't think."

KAEL: "You're sure about that?"
```

The player laughs—Kael is very weird. But Kael is serious:

```
KAEL: "I think it should have a name. Something that fits it. The pattern on it looks like... like wind caught in water. So maybe... Windwater. Or the pattern looks like a wing, too. Wingstone. Or just... Keeper."

PLAYER: "Keeper of what?"

KAEL: "Of memories. Your memories, now. It's keeping them safe."
```

This is very Kael: ascribing intention and consciousness to inanimate things, finding the poetry in observation, making ordinary objects significant through attention.

**What This Moment Does:**
- Shows Kael's way of thinking (everything is aware, everything matters, everything deserves naming)
- Makes the stone feel special—it's not just any stone, it's *Keeper*
- Plants the idea that consciousness or awareness is broader than just people
- Ties into the later revelation about magic being aware—Kael intuited something true

## Memory 4: Kael's Concern (Night Before the Attack)

### Context
This scene happens the night before the coordinated assault. It's crucial because it shows Kael as someone who *notices* and *worries*, not just observes passively.

**Setting:** The player's home or the healing house. Evening. Kael comes to find the player, which is unusual—Kael typically waits to be sought out.

**What Happens:**

Kael seems unsettled:

```
KAEL: "Have you felt it? The way the air is different?"

PLAYER: "Different how?"

KAEL: "Heavy. Like something is moving under the ground. Or watching. I don't know how to explain it. The magic has been acting strange all day. Withdrawn, almost. Like it's scared."
```

The player dismisses this (the village is safe, there's been no warning, Kael is imaginative):

```
PLAYER: "You're just noticing normal things and making them weird."

KAEL: "Maybe. But ask your parents. Ask the brother. I think they feel it too. They're just not saying it because what would we do about it?"
```

Kael leaves, still unsettled. The player forgets this conversation—they're busy with other things.

**What This Moment Does:**
- Shows Kael as someone who *notices* danger before it's obvious
- Plants the idea that the attack wasn't random—something was coming, something felt it arriving
- Makes Kael's death more tragic: they saw the threat coming and no one listened
- Adds to the player's guilt later (Kael tried to warn them)

## The Death Moment (Not Graphically Detailed, but Present)

### Setting: The Attack

During the coordinated assault, the player and Kael are separated. The player is protecting themselves and the brother is pulling them away. Kael is somewhere else—maybe near the workshop or trying to reach other people they care about.

**What Happens:**

The player discovers Kael's body afterward—during the "discovering losses one-by-one" sequence from Session 12 (the village attack scene). The discovery is:

```
The player sees a familiar shape near the workshop entrance. The easy recognition
before the reality catches up.

Kael's body is still. Their hand is still open, like they were reaching for something
or someone. The expression on their face is peace, not pain—Kael's particular kind
of peace, like they understood something in their last moment and accepted it.

The stone the player was given sits nearby. Kael must have been carrying it—
or perhaps it fell from the player's pocket when they fled. Either way, it's there.

The player picks it up.
```

This is not a detailed gore scene. It's the emotional recognition: Kael was here. Kael tried to do something. Kael is gone.

**What This Means:**
- Kael's death is specific (near the workshop, at peace)
- The stone becomes a physical anchor for grief
- The player has a concrete object to carry forward
- The player's guilt is reinforced: Kael warned them, Kael tried to do something, Kael is dead

## Thematic Significance of Kael's Loss

### For the Player Emotionally:
- Parents = structural loss (they taught how to live)
- Brother = relational loss (he chose to leave)
- Kael = philosophical loss (they taught *why* to wonder)

The three losses hit differently. Kael's loss is the loss of a particular way of seeing the world.

### For the Game Mechanically:
- Kael's observation that "magic is aware" becomes relevant in:
  - Hollowing (magic responding to desperation)
  - Ambient resonance (magic organizing without intention)
  - Corruption (magic carving you out based on what you feel)
- Kael's warning about something coming creates narrative weight (there was a sign, the village missed it)

### For the Four Paths:
- **Community Path:** The player honors Kael's way of noticing by taking time to understand the village's deeper nature before acting
- **Knowledge Path:** The player pursues understanding the way Kael pursued it—through observation and wonder, not dominance
- **Wandering Path:** The player carries Kael's stone and their way of seeing as they leave
- **Corruption Path:** The player abandons Kael's gentle way of knowing in favor of forcing answers from magic
