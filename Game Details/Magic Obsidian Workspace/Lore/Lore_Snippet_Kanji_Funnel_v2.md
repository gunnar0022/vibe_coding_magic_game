# LORE SNIPPET — The Kanji Funnel: How Humans Cast Magic

## The Interface

Magic is cast through symbols. Specifically, through kanji — characters that originated in the old world's writing systems and became something far more than language when magic arrived.

To cast, a person holds a symbol in their mind, focuses on it, and channels ambient magic through it. The symbol acts as a funnel: magic flows in from the surrounding environment, passes through the shape of the symbol's meaning, and emerges as an effect. The process isn't physically demanding. It doesn't require gestures, spoken words, or ritual. You need to know the symbol, hold it clearly, and let the magic move through it. That's all.

This simplicity is part of what makes magic both accessible and dangerous. The barrier to casting isn't talent or training — it's knowledge. If you know the symbol, you can cast the spell. The scarcity isn't ability. It's information.


## The Funnel Mechanic

When a person holds a kanji, meaning is not required, in their mind and channels magic, the symbol shapes what comes through. The ambient mana in the environment flows toward the caster, enters the conceptual shape of the symbol, and is output as a corresponding effect. The caster isn't creating the magic. They're directing it — giving it a form to pass through, the way water takes the shape of whatever channel it flows down.

This is why the mana pool matters. The funnel draws from whatever is available. In a healthy environment, full of life and regenerating mana, the magic flows freely. The world gives, the symbol shapes, the effect emerges. Safe. Sustainable. The funnel takes only what the environment offers.

In a drained environment, the funnel doesn't stop working. It still pulls. But there's nothing external left to pull from — so it draws from the next available source: the caster. Health, vitality, being. The funnel doesn't distinguish between ambient mana and the caster's own life force. It just needs something to flow through it. This is the mechanical root of hollowing — the funnel keeps working exactly as designed, but the source has changed from the world to the self, and the caster may not feel the difference until the damage is done.

## Combinatorial Casting

Two kanji can be held simultaneously, creating a combined funnel. The magic passes through both shapes at once, and the intersection of their meanings produces a unique, unified effect. This isn't addition — symbol A plus symbol B doesn't produce effect A plus effect B. It produces effect C, something new that emerges from the conceptual space *between* the two symbols. The combined funnel is a shape neither symbol creates alone.

This is where experimentation lives. A player who discovers a new kanji can try pairing it with every symbol they already know. Most combinations will produce coherent, if unexpected, results — the magic reads the combined meaning and does something sensible with it. Some combinations will surprise. A few may be dangerous, because the conceptual intersection isn't always intuitive, and magic doesn't care whether the caster anticipated the output. You proposed the shape. Magic filled it.

The combinatorial space is enormous. Even a modest collection of kanji produces a large number of possible pairings, and each pairing is functionally a new spell. This means that no single community has explored the full possibility space. One village might have discovered a pairing that another village has never tried, simply because their people resonated with different symbols. Knowledge is fragmented, regional, and incomplete — not because people are hoarding secrets (though some are), but because the space is too vast for any one group to map, and discovery itself is personal and unpredictable.

## Discovery and Sharing

The asymmetry at the heart of the magic system: discovery is deeply personal and non-trivial, but sharing is instant and effortless.

A person might spend months or years living a certain way before the moment of resonance arrives and a symbol crystallizes in their awareness. The experience is transformative — a moment of soul that can't be faked, rushed, or repeated on demand. But once they have the symbol, sharing it is as simple as drawing the shape and showing it to someone else. The recipient doesn't need to undergo their own moment of resonance. They just need to learn the form. Once they know the shape, they can hold it in their mind and channel through it like anyone else.

This makes every person who has discovered a symbol through resonance a potential source for everyone they meet. A craftsman who resonated with 鉄 and can't do anything useful with it alone might share it with someone who already knows 火 — and the combination produces something neither of them could have found independently. The system rewards sharing even as the knowledge economy creates incentives to hoard.

## What the Characters Don't Know

The in-world understanding of this system is almost nonexistent. Magic has been accessible for about a year. People know that certain symbols produce effects when held in the mind. People know that these symbols sometimes arrive in moments of intense experience, and sometimes appear etched into the natural world. People know that combining symbols produces new effects. Beyond that, everything is theory, superstition, and guesswork.

The deeper architecture — that magic bonded to old-world kanji during the fall, that it spent generations gestating inside those symbolic structures, that the recent trickle of discovery is the breaking of a storm that's been building since before anyone alive was born — is unknown to every character in the game. The mentor researcher might be working toward pieces of it. The companion researcher might stumble onto fragments. But the full picture is designer-level knowledge. The player feels it working without being told how it works.

Communities have their own theories: the symbols are divine words, they're elemental keys, they're the world's own writing. Everyone is partially right. Nobody has the full picture. And the symbols keep appearing — in moments of human intensity, on lightning-struck trees, at the bases of ancient oaks — with no explanation that anyone can agree on.
