## CORE LORE PRINCIPLES

### Magic and the World
Magic entered the world suddenly and catastrophically. Technology failed instantly and permanently. Society collapsed overnight. Many died. Others fled to the wild.

By the time of the game — two to three generations later — magic is simply *normal*. The protagonist was born into it. It's not miraculous or feared, just part of the landscape. The world has stabilized into loose villages, isolated communities, and fragmented knowledge. Long-distance communication is rare. Travel is dangerous. Many don't know where their nearest neighbors are.

Magic appears to have organized spontaneously into something resembling a primitive form of life. It doesn't reason or judge. It responds. When invoked, it amplifies what already exists within living things, pushing them toward their natural extremes. Discovery is ongoing and global. No single community has complete understanding. What one village treats as foundational truth, another dismisses as superstition.

### How Magic Works (The Giving and Taking)
Every area in the world contains a large, shared pool of environmental mana, generated by the life in that region. All magic users — player and enemies — draw from this same pool. **Using magic from a full environment is safe.** The world gives freely when it has something to give.

The pool is finite but regenerates. Life breathes it back. The land always recovers. But the pool can be drained faster than it recovers, especially when multiple users draw from it simultaneously or when someone casts relentlessly without pause.

**The danger begins when you keep taking after there's nothing left.** When the environment is drained and you continue casting, you draw from yourself — your health, your being. Health-to-mana conversion is always available, consistent, and designed to feel convenient. The game never makes it feel desperate in the moment. That's what makes it dangerous. The cost accumulates invisibly.

### Corruption: The Hollowing
Magic itself is not the poison. Corruption begins when you take from yourself. Each time you cast from health — from your own being — it carves a gap in your soul. That gap is filled with *nothing*. Emptiness.

**That emptiness gets filled by whatever is loudest inside you.** Grief, pride, hunger, fear, wrath, ambition — whatever your dominant drives are rush in to fill the hollow space. This is the amplification. Magic doesn't inject corruption. It creates the void. Your own nature fills it.

The process is gradual. Invisible. You don't feel it happening. You just become *more* of what you already were, and less of everything else. The mana bar isn't just a resource tracker — it's the world telling you to stop. To wait. To let it breathe. The player who respects that boundary is safe. The player who pushes past it, spending health to keep casting, is choosing to hollow themselves out. The game doesn't punish you for using magic. It punishes you for refusing to stop.

### The Corruption Spectrum
The complexity of the transformation mirrors the complexity of the motive that fills the void.

A desperate, hungry group that hollowed themselves out trying to survive becomes something simple — feral humanoid creatures with one drive, one purpose, stripped of everything else. Bestial. Easy to recognize. Openly hostile. These are the remnants of simple desperation.

A person carrying grief, pride, protectiveness, wrath, and genuine love — if they hollow enough of themselves out, those drives expand to fill the space but retain their complexity. The result can still think. Still plan. Still feel twisted versions of what it felt before. Not a monster — a tragedy with teeth. The most dangerous corrupted beings in this world are the ones that can still talk to you.

The seven deadly sins work as a design lens, not a literal in-game system. The dominant drive determines the flavor of transformation. Hunger becomes gluttony. Fear becomes something that hoards and hides. Ambition becomes something that can never stop acquiring. Pride becomes something that must dominate. More complex characters, holding multiple drives simultaneously, produce more complex and more terrifying results.

### The World Is Regenerative. People Break.
There is no "spreading darkness" across the map. Drained areas recover as life breathes mana back. Corruption zones are temporary. The land always heals.

The danger is entirely behavioral. Settlements don't fall because corruption spread to them — they fall because people made choices under pressure and refused to stop taking. The land under them recovered after they were gone. The world is fundamentally generous. The tragedy is always human.

## LORE IMPLICATIONS TO TRACK

- **Hollowing is the unified theory.** Taking from yourself when there's nothing left creates the void. Your nature fills it. This connects corruption, the brother, the attackers, and the entire bestiary.
- The mana bar is a warning system. The world telling you when to stop.
- Restrained, sustainable magic use is safer not because pacifism is rewarded, but because the lifestyle doesn't create the void. Less taking, less hollowing.
- The brother's corruption is invisible because his motives are genuinely mixed — love and wrath coexist, and the hollowing amplifies both without distinguishing between them.
- The mentor researcher's collaborative body of work makes the corruption ending's betrayal deeply personal — you're destroying something you helped create.
- The corruption spectrum means every monster the player fights was once a person who emptied themselves out. The simple ones were driven by simple needs. The complex ones had complex hearts.
- Contemplative magic (meditation, utility) mirrors the lore's "resonances" — the world aligning with perception, offering rather than being taken from.
