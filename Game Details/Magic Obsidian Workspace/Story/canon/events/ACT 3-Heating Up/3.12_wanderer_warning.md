# The Wanderer's Warning

## Placement
- Late Act 3. Rahn's final appearance before the endgame. Should land after the player has seen Sael slipping and ideally after the complex corrupted encounter.

## Who's Involved
- The player
- Rahn

## What Happens
- Rahn appears at a destination the player reaches. As always, he's already there — he doesn't follow, he arrives.
- He's seen Sael. Or he's seen what Sael is becoming. He recognizes the pattern from a lifetime of watching people destroy themselves through refusal to stop.
- His warning isn't dramatic. It's resigned. Quiet. The certainty of someone who's watched this play out before and knows the ending.
- He draws the parallel to Ven — the person he loved who worked themselves to death because stopping felt like surrender. Sael is doing the same thing with magic instead of labor.
- The player has to decide what Rahn's detachment means: is he wise or broken? Is his peace earned or bought at the cost of everyone he could have helped?

## Key Beats
- Rahn is right about the pattern. That's what makes him tragic — being right doesn't save anyone.
- He won't act. He's telling the player because the player still might. But he won't go with them.
- This is the last quiet moment before the endgame tightens. Rahn's stillness contrasts with the urgency of everything that's coming.
- If the player asks for help, Rahn's refusal is gentle but absolute.
