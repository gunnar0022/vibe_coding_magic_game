# The Home Village

## Locked Endpoints

- About fifteen families. Humble.
- They farm, they hunt, they share. No currency — only cooperation.
- Central plaza where people eat together. A city hall type gathering space.
- Small and it works. It's home.
- Damaged by the attack — about a quarter lost (lives, structures, resources).
- Survives. Wounded, not destroyed. There is something real to rebuild.

## Narrative Function

- The community path takes place here. The player chooses to stay where it happened.
- The brother can't stay — the village is daily reminders of what was taken.
- Community path = thriving village over time. Other paths = varies.

## Overview

The village is not a planned settlement. It evolved. About eighty years ago (in the pre-collapse era), a family found a sheltered valley in the foothills. Good water. Cleared land suitable for crops. Forest for hunting and materials. They stayed. Others came. Slowly, organically, a settlement grew without formal structure. When the Fall happened, the village was already accustomed to collective decision-making and shared resources.

The layout reflects this organic growth: buildings cluster around a central plaza, but not in neat rows. There's a logic to it (families stay near families, functions near compatible functions), but it's the logic of habit, not planning.

## The Central Plaza

### Function
The plaza is the village's gathering heart. Morning meetings. Evening meals. Celebrations. Disputes. Processing catch from hunts. Community decisions. It's an open-air assembly space, roughly thirty paces across, ringed by the largest and most-used buildings.

### Physical Description

**Ground:** Packed earth, worn smooth by forty years of footfall. In spring it turns to mud. In autumn, fallen leaves. In winter, sometimes snow, but the valley stays relatively mild. The plaza has a slight slope toward its eastern edge—water runs there in rain.

**Surroundings:**
- **North:** The meeting hall (largest structure, semi-permanent roof, open sides)
- **South:** The food storage building (sturdy, cool, built partially into the hillside)
- **East:** The workshop (where Thorne did his work—carpentry, tool repair, building materials storage)
- **West:** The healing house (where Elsa worked and lived with the player and brother)

**Central Feature:** A large flat stone, roughly person-sized, stands in the plaza's center. It's called the Speaking Stone. Village meetings happen with someone standing on it. It's not a formal podium—just a raised place where one voice can reach everyone.

**Sensory Details:**
- **Sounds:** Morning plaza is alive—children playing, people greeting each other, someone working at the workshop's hearth. Evening is quieter, more meditative. Voices carry. A conversation across the plaza is overheard by everyone.
- **Smell:** Complex. In autumn, wood smoke from cooking fires. In spring, fresh growth and mud. Year-round: animal hides from the workshop, herbs hanging to dry outside the healing house, bread baking from the communal oven (located near the food storage building).
- **Light:** The plaza is open to sky, so light changes throughout the day. Morning sun comes from the east (workshop side). Evening light is long and golden from the west (healing house side).

## The Buildings: Their Purpose and Location

### 1. The Meeting Hall (North Side)

**What it is:** The largest public structure. Roughly forty paces long, twenty wide. Partial roof (the winter half is weatherproofed; the summer half is open-air). The sides are open to the plaza—no walls, just posts.

**What it's used for:**
- Village assemblies (everyone fits, barely)
- Meals during bad weather
- Storage of community tools and supplies
- Occasional shelter for travelers

**Built by:** Thorne (with help from other families). About fifteen years old. Shows his craftsmanship—the joints are tight, the structure is sound.

**How the attack affected it:** The attackers didn't target the structure specifically, but the chaos of combat left marks. Post scorch marks where a hollowed creature burned. Damage to the open sides (some posts cracked). Blood was cleaned, but the emotional weight remains—this is where people died.

---

### 2. The Healing House (West Side)

**What it is:** A structure built specifically for Elsa's work. About thirty paces deep, split into sections: living quarters in front (where the player and brother grew up), working space in the middle (benches for stitching, laying out herbs, examining patients), storage in back (medicines, bandages, knowledge written on pressed bark or leather).

**What makes it special:**
- The only building with a dedicated hearth and ventilation for smoke
- Large windows (Elsa insisted on light for detailed work)
- Built-in shelving for herbs and medicines
- A sturdy door that closes (privacy for birthing, serious injuries, sensitive patient work)
- Small garden out back where Kess grows healing herbs

**Built by:** Thorne (naturally), with input from Elsa. About twenty years old.

**How the attack affected it:**
- Several attackers converged here (people sheltered inside during the attack). The building was breached, but the structure itself held.
- One wall has deep gouges from struggling (people defending the trapped inside). The gouges are still visible.
- Inside, bloodstains remain in places (attempts to clean them failed; the wood absorbed it).
- Elsa died here, tending wounded people even as she bled out from her own injuries.
- This is the hardest space for the player to return to early game.

---

### 3. The Workshop (East Side)

**What it is:** Thorne's domain. Roughly twenty paces square. Partially roofed (the work area is open, the tool storage section has a roof). Multiple hearths for woodwork and tool-making. Benches, saws, chisels, wood in various stages of processing. The smell of woodsmoke and fresh-cut timber hangs here permanently.

**What it stores:**
- Tools for every family
- Materials for repairs (wood, leather straps, metal scrap)
- Projects in progress (frames for new structures, furniture repairs)
- Thorne's design sketches (pressed into bark, secured in a wooden box)

**Built by:** Thorne. Expanded and modified several times as his work grew more sophisticated.

**How the attack affected it:**
- Largely spared (fighters didn't concentrate here)
- But Thorne died defending others near the workshop's entrance
- His current project (a new roof frame for a family whose structure was damaged) sits unfinished
- His tool collection is intact, but no one else knows how he organized them
- His design sketches remain, but without his presence, they're just drawings

**Current situation:** Jenna (Thane's daughter) is attempting to learn Thorne's craft, but she's lost without him. Basic repairs she can manage. Anything complex is beyond her current skill. The village is learning what a single person's expertise costs when they're gone.

---

### 4. The Food Storage Building (South Side)

**What it is:** Built into the hillside (cool interior even in summer). Stone foundation, wooden structure above. Divided into sections: root vegetable storage (underground chamber, naturally cool), preserved meat (hanging, smoked), grain stores (sealed containers), preserved vegetables and fruits (in oil or brine). A small cooking area where basic food processing happens.

**What makes it critical:** Food is life. Loss of the storage building would be catastrophic. Everything the village depends on for winter is here.

**Built by:** Thorne and Kai together (hunters needed input on meat storage). About twelve years old.

**How the attack affected it:**
- Deliberately avoided by the attackers (no one burned it; no one targeted it)
- Possibly because the attackers needed food themselves
- Or possibly because they were organized enough to recognize that destroying the food store would make the village unsustainable
- This ambiguity hangs over the village: were these attackers people (who needed food)? Or entities so alien they had no interest in resources?

## The Residential Areas

### The Player's Home (Near the Healing House)

The player grew up here—the building attached to the healing house where Elsa and Thorne lived with the player and brother. This space is *deeply* familiar to the player.

**What it contains:**
- Two sleeping areas (one for parents, one for children)
- A work area where Thorne sketched designs and stored his personal tools
- A small hearth for cooking
- Storage for family belongings

**Sensory markers the player remembers:**
- Thorne's leather apron hanging by the door (still there)
- The smell of Elsa's herb mixtures
- The particular creak of the floor at the threshold (the player has heard this sound their entire life)
- The light from the east-facing window where Elsa organized her work

**Current state:** The building was damaged in the attack but still stands. Entering it early game is emotionally fraught. The player's family is gone. Everything in the space is a memory.

---

### Other Residential Clusters

Families live near families. Several small clusters of two to four buildings each, spaced around the outer edge of the settlement. Each cluster has:
- Living quarters
- Small storage sheds
- Often a workspace appropriate to that family's role (Kai and Merith have a smoking shed, Kess has her healing garden)

These clusters are separated enough to provide privacy but close enough that community is impossible to avoid. The architecture enforces the village's values: together, but not stifling.

## Seasonal Changes

### Spring (Melt & Planting)
- The plaza becomes muddy
- Families emerge from winter quarters into workshops and gardens
- Hunting expeditions restart
- The village feels like it's waking up

### Summer (Growth & Vigilance)
- Long daylight extends work into evening
- Food is abundant (gardens producing, hunting is good)
- The village feels secure
- This is when the attack happened (abundance makes peace feel stable)

### Autumn (Harvest & Preparation)
- The food storage building is filled (hunt, harvest, preserve)
- The village works together to prepare for winter
- Sense of urgency and purpose
- Travel is still possible, but dangerous (weather coming)

### Winter (Scarcity & Intimacy)
- The village is quieter, inward-focused
- Frequent communal meals (shared heat, shared resources)
- Stories and knowledge-sharing happen (no other entertainment)
- The distance between buildings feels bigger (paths are dangerous in snow)

## The Attack's Physical Marks

### Visible Damage

- **The healing house:** Broken sections, deep gouges, blood stains
- **Plaza:** Ash and burn marks where structures burned or fire was used
- **Workshop:** Thorne's blood, visible still on the entrance threshold (someone tried to clean it but gave up)
- **Food storage:** Notably *unmarked*—which creates a dark question

### Emotional Damage

The village survives, but the damage is not just physical. Movement through the village is navigation of memory:
- The place where Kael died (near the workshop)
- The place where Thorne fell (plaza entrance)
- The healing house where Elsa died
- The family homes that are now empty

For the player, coming back to the village early game is a map of loss. The community path asks: can this place become something other than a tomb?

## How the Village Changes by Path

### Community Path
- Gradually, visible work begins
- The workshop gets new hands (Jenna learns faster with player's help)
- The healing house becomes a collaborative space (player and Kess figure things out together)
- The village feels like it's *rebuilding*, not just surviving
- By late game, the scars are visible but the village is visibly thriving

### Wandering Path
- The village is maintained, but it's a holding pattern
- The player is gone, so the sense of shared rebuilding doesn't happen
- By late game, the village has survived but hasn't thrived
- There's an underlying wistfulness—the player left, and the village feels abandoned

### Knowledge Path
- The village is secondary to the research camp
- But knowledge from the camp filters back (healing techniques, magic theory)
- By late game, the village is stable but different—more informed, less isolated
- The healing house becomes a place where old knowledge and new understanding merge

### Corruption/Brother Paths
- The village is a static emotional anchor
- The player's connection to home becomes a painful contrast to their current state
- By late game, the village represents what the player has lost or abandoned

## The Central Plaza as Emotional Compass

Throughout the game, the player returns to the plaza (or thinks about it). The plaza's state and feeling shifts:

- **Early game (just after attack):** Quiet, damaged, grieving. The Speaking Stone stands empty.
- **Mid-game (on active path):** Slowly coming alive. People are talking, planning.
- **Late game (path-dependent):**
  - Community: The plaza is full. Celebrations, gathered work, laughter.
  - Wandering: Quieter. People living their lives, but without the player. An ache of absence.
  - Knowledge: Intellectual energy. Discussion of new ideas, theories, discoveries.
  - Corruption: The plaza is just a memory. The player no longer belongs here.
