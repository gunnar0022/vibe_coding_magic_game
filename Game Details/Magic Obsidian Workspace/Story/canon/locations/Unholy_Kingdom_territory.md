# The Unholy Kingdom — Territory

## Geographic Position

The Unholy Kingdom sits north of the Holy Enclave. Both communities occupy the far north of the known world — well beyond the Crossroads region where the main story takes place. The two settlements are each other's closest neighbors, but "closest" is relative. Significant distance separates them — days of travel through difficult terrain. Close enough to trade (historically), close enough to threaten (now), far enough that neither can easily overwhelm the other.

The missionaries travel south from the Holy Enclave, away from the Kingdom's territory, to reach the Crossroads communities. The Kingdom has no reason to pursue them in that direction — their attention is focused on the Enclave and their own internal experiments, not on distant southern settlements they don't know exist.

## The Land

The Kingdom occupies a broad valley system in the northern highlands. The terrain is productive — river-fed lowlands for agriculture, forested slopes for timber and game, natural barriers (ridgelines, dense forest, river gorges) that provide defensive advantage.

This was always good land. That's part of why this community thrived when others merely survived. The valley provided what most post-Fall settlements lacked: reliable water, fertile soil, natural shelter from weather, and enough ecological diversity to support both farming and livestock. The old-world settler who brought practical knowledge chose well — or got lucky.

### Pre-corruption character:
- Organized farmland in the valley floor — crops, pastures, livestock pens
- Forested slopes providing timber, game, and wild resources
- A central settlement cluster — not a single village but a constellation of buildings and work areas spread along the river, connected by maintained paths
- Evidence of metalworking (forge, tools, basic hardware)
- The most developed infrastructure of any community in the north — fences, irrigation channels, storage buildings, animal shelters

### Post-corruption character:
- The farmland still functions. Hollowed servants maintain it mechanically — planting, harvesting, tending livestock with empty precision. The crops grow. The animals are fed. The output continues. It's agriculture performed by beings who no longer care about eating but understand that the rulers require supply.
- The central settlement has changed. The rulers have claimed the best structures. New construction — crude but purposeful — houses the experiments. Areas that were once communal spaces are now restricted.
- The forge still operates, producing tools and weapons. The metalworking knowledge survived the hollowing because it's a skill, not a moral quality. Corrupted rulers with ambition want better weapons. Hollowed servants with compliance operate the bellows.
- The natural beauty of the valley is untouched. The river still runs. The forests still grow. The mana pools are deep — three generations of feedback loop in a productive ecosystem. This is part of what makes the Kingdom dangerous: they're sitting on some of the richest mana reserves in the north, and they're not shy about draining them.

## The Border Zone

Between the Holy Enclave and the Unholy Kingdom lies rough, hilly terrain — not impassable, but difficult. Dense forest, steep ridges, seasonal flooding in the lowlands between. This is the buffer that has kept the standstill viable. Neither side can march an army through it quickly or quietly. Skirmishes happen at the edges. Raiding parties from the Kingdom test the Enclave's perimeter. Enclave patrols push back. But a full-scale assault from either direction would require committing forces through terrain that favors the defender.

The Enclave has used this geography to their advantage, establishing watch points and patrol routes through the border hills. The Kingdom hasn't pushed hard because they're not ready — they want a decisive advantage, not a war of attrition through bad terrain.

## Narrative Function

- **Not visited during the main game.** The Unholy Kingdom is too far north, too dangerous, and too disconnected from the Crossroads region for the player to reach during the main story.
- **Known only through the missionaries' testimony.** Everything the player learns about this place comes filtered through the Holy Enclave's theological lens. The missionaries describe what they've seen and heard, but their interpretation is shaped by their faith.
- **Potential post-game content.** If the story expands beyond the main arc, the Kingdom becomes a destination — the player traveling north to confront the most organized and dangerous corrupted threat in the world. The scale would be dramatically different from anything in the main game.
- **Thematic mirror.** The Kingdom is what happens when a successful community gets magic and uses it without restraint. The player's home village was attacked by people who hollowed from simple desperation. The Kingdom hollowed from entitlement and ambition. Same mechanic, vastly different scale and sophistication.
