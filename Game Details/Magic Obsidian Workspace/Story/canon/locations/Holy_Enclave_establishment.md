# The Holy Enclave — Settlement

## Geographic Position

The Holy Enclave occupies a defensible position in the far north — well beyond the Crossroads region. South of the Unholy Kingdom, separated by rough, hilly terrain that serves as a natural buffer zone.

The Enclave is the furthest organized human community the player learns about during the main game. Everything the player knows about it comes through the missionaries' descriptions — filtered through theology, colored by devotion, but conveying a genuine picture of a large, functional, faith-driven society.

## The Settlement

### Scale

Significantly larger than anything in the Crossroads region. A few hundred people, possibly more. The Enclave isn't a village — it's closer to a small town, with enough population to support specialized roles: farmers, builders, priests, soldiers, craftspeople. The Crossroads communities have maybe fifty to a hundred people each. The Enclave dwarfs them.

This scale is itself a revelation when the missionaries describe it. The player's world has been defined by small, isolated communities. Learning that a settlement this large exists changes the player's understanding of what's possible in the post-Fall world.

### Layout

- **A central temple or gathering hall** — the spiritual heart of the community. Where worship happens, where decisions are made, where the community comes together. Not grand in the old-world sense, but the largest structure any of these people have ever built. Functional, maintained with care.
- **Residential clusters** spreading outward from the center — families, individuals, organized by some combination of function and social standing. More structure than the Crossroads villages, which grew organically. The Enclave was planned, at least partially, around the temple.
- **Agricultural zones** — farms, gardens, livestock areas. Large enough to sustain the population with surplus. Not as advanced as the Unholy Kingdom's pre-corruption agriculture (no old-world knowledge head start), but functional and well-maintained.
- **Military installations** — training grounds, improvised weapon storage, crude watch posts along the northern perimeter. These are newer additions, built in the past year in response to the Kingdom threat. Before magic arrived, the Enclave didn't need a military.
- **The northern border** — patrol routes, early warning systems. The terrain between the Enclave and the Kingdom is rough and wooded. The Enclave uses this geography defensively, maintaining watch points on high ground and patrol circuits through the border hills.

## The Border Zone

Between the Enclave and the Unholy Kingdom: rough, hilly, heavily forested terrain. Days of difficult travel. Steep ridges, seasonal flooding in the lowlands, dense growth from three generations of magical amplification.

This terrain is the primary reason the standstill has held. Neither side can move a large force through it quickly or quietly. The Enclave patrols it defensively. The Kingdom probes it with raiding parties. Neither commits to a full assault because the terrain makes that suicidal for the attacker.

The Enclave's military advantage is here — established positions, and fight defensively on ground that favors them. On open ground, the Kingdom's numbers and aggressive casting would likely overwhelm them.

## Mana Environment

The Enclave's mana ecology is healthy. Their theology-driven restraint in casting means they don't drain their local pools. The surrounding wilderness — amplified wild, three generations of feedback loop — provides deep ambient mana. The community draws from it sustainably, not because they understand mana ecology, but because their faith tells them that overcasting is blasphemy.

This accidental alignment between theology and mana mechanics is part of what makes the Enclave functional. They're safe because they're reverent, and they're reverent because they believe being reverent is what keeps Her present. The circular logic protects them from the truth: it's not Her presence. It's just a pool that regenerates when you don't drain it.

## Narrative Function

- **Not visited during the main game.** Too far north, too disconnected from the Crossroads region.
- **Known only through missionary testimony.** The player builds a mental picture from descriptions, filtered through theology.
- **Represents organized human society at a scale the player hasn't seen.** The revelation that communities this large exist expands the player's sense of the world dramatically.
- **The "safe" version of the north.** Contrasts with the Unholy Kingdom — same region, same general prosperity, opposite outcomes. Faith-driven restraint vs. ambition-driven excess. The Enclave survived because their theology accidentally protected them from hollowing. The Kingdom fell because their pragmatism had no guardrails.
