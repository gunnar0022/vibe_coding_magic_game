# The Unholy Kingdom

## Overview

A former ally of the Holy Enclave, once the most prosperous human settlement in the known north. Now a hierarchy of intelligent corrupted beings ruling over a subjugated population of hollowed servants. The most dangerous organized threat in the world — not because they're mindless, but because they can think, plan, and experiment.

The Holy Enclave calls them the Damned. They don't call themselves anything. They don't need a name. They just take.

---

## Origin: Before Magic (~50 years post-Fall to ~1 year ago)

### The Head Start

An early settler of the old world survived the Fall carrying no working technology, but something arguably more valuable: practical knowledge. Not blueprints or manuals — just expertise that lived in someone's head. Things like:

- Using materials efficiently for construction (fence-building techniques, load-bearing principles)
- Cross-pollinating plants to produce hardier or more productive crops
- Constructing a primitive bellows to create a forge capable of producing crude metal tools
- Basic animal husbandry — not just hunting, but keeping and breeding livestock

These aren't revolutionary ideas. They're the kind of knowledge that would take a community decades to rediscover through trial and error. Having them from the start meant this settlement skipped years of fumbling. They had better fences, better crops, better tools, and eventually livestock — advantages that compounded over generations.

### The Compounding Effect

By the time the rest of the world was still stabilizing, this community was thriving. Not dramatically — they weren't building castles. But they had surplus food, functional metal tools, organized animal husbandry, and a population large enough to specialize labor. Some people could stop farming and start governing, crafting, or exploring because others produced enough for everyone.

This surplus bred hierarchy. Not tyrannical at first — just practical. Someone had to decide how surplus was allocated. Someone had to resolve disputes about grazing land. Someone had to organize defense against amplified predators. A leadership class emerged naturally, and because the community's success was real and visible, the leaders were respected. They earned their authority through competence.

But competence-based authority, over decades, drifts toward entitlement. The people on top began to believe they were on top because they deserved to be. The people on the bottom accepted this because the system worked. By the time magic arrived, the hierarchy was deep-rooted and unquestioned.

### Relationship with the Holy Enclave

For roughly 50 years, these two northern communities were allies. Not close — separated by significant distance — but their closest neighbors. They traded occasionally, shared warnings about predator activity, and maintained a general understanding that they were the two success stories of the far north. Different cultures, different organizing principles, but mutual respect born from being the only two communities that had genuinely thrived.

## The Corruption Timeline (~1 year ago to present)

### Month 1-2: Discovery and Divergence

Magic becomes accessible globally. Both communities discover it almost simultaneously.

The Holy Enclave interprets magic as divine — a gift from Her, channeled through prayer. Their response is reverence, caution, worship. They build theology around it.

This community interprets magic as a tool. Their leadership class, already accustomed to leveraging advantages, sees magic as the next advantage to leverage. They approach it with the same pragmatism that built their forges and their fences: figure out how it works, use it to consolidate what we have, expand what we can control.

The leadership casts the most. They have the leisure, the ambition, and the belief that they should be the ones wielding this new power. They cast aggressively — in council chambers, in the forges, in the fields. They push hard because pushing hard is what built this community.

### Month 3-4: The First Hollowing

The leaders hollow first. Not all at once — individually, one by one, each pushing past the local mana pool's limits because there's always one more thing to try, one more advantage to secure.

Their dominant drives are complex: ambition, entitlement, the conviction that they *deserve* to rule, pride in what they've built, and genuine belief that their community's success validates their authority. When the hollowing carves voids in them, these drives rush in to fill the space. The result is not feral. It's intelligent. Calculating. Still capable of speech, planning, and social manipulation — but stripped of empathy, restraint, and the ability to see other people as anything other than resources.

They don't lose their positions. They weaponize them. The existing hierarchy means the people below them have no mechanism to resist. The rulers were already in charge. Now they're in charge and they've lost the parts of themselves that would have stopped them from exploiting that fully.

### Month 4-6: The Subjugation Cascade

The population below the corrupted leaders begins to change. Not from overcasting — most of them aren't casting at all. They're changing because they're living under increasingly inhuman masters and the stress is unbearable.

Fear. Submission. The inability to resist. These become the dominant drives for the subjugated population. When the environmental mana gets drained by the leaders' aggressive casting and the subjects are forced to work in depleted zones, their own hollowing begins — slow, passive, driven not by ambition but by terror.

**This produces a new point on the corruption spectrum.** These people don't become feral beasts (their fear is more complex than simple survival desperation). They don't become cunning schemers (their drives aren't ambitious enough). They become something in between: hollowed servants. People-shaped tools that follow orders because compliance is the only drive left. Functional enough to serve. Empty enough to never rebel.

This happened organically at first. The rulers didn't engineer it — they just noticed that their people were becoming more obedient, less resistant, less likely to question. The hollowing of the servant class was a side effect of living under monsters, not a deliberate program.

### Month 6-8: Recognition and Escalation

The corrupted leaders notice the pattern. Their servants are hollowing in a specific, useful way. This is the inflection point — the moment where an accidental consequence becomes a deliberate strategy.

They begin experimenting. Not yet with the sophistication they'll eventually develop, but with the crude curiosity of intelligent beings who've discovered they can shape others:

- Forcing subjects to cast in depleted zones to accelerate the submission-hollowing
- Observing what different emotional states produce when hollowing occurs
- Testing whether varying conditions (isolation, fear, exhaustion) change the outcome

The hierarchy cements. Corrupted rulers on top, hollowed servants below, and a shrinking middle of still-human subjects who haven't yet been broken — living in increasing terror, knowing what's coming for them.

### Month 8-10: The Break with the Enclave

Contact between the two communities deteriorates rapidly. A trader from the Holy Enclave visits and doesn't return. Then another. A gathering party from the Enclave is attacked near the border — by people who are visibly changed, slightly deformed, but organized and armed.

The Holy Enclave pieces it together through a series of escalating incidents:
- Missing traders (assumed lost to wildlife, then suspected otherwise)
- A trader who does return, shaken, reporting that the community "felt wrong"
- The outright attack on gatherers by recognizably changed people
- The theological conclusion: they rejected Her word, used Her gift for selfish gain, and were punished. The transformation is divine judgment.

The Enclave's interpretation is wrong about mechanism but right about trajectory. The people they once traded with have become something dangerous. The border becomes hostile.

### Month 10-Present: The Standstill and the Experiments

The Unholy Kingdom (as the Enclave now thinks of them, though they'd use the word "Damned") has stabilized into a functioning horror:

**The hierarchy:** Corrupted rulers (intelligent, ambitious, stripped of empathy) command hollowed servants (compliant, empty, functional). A dwindling number of still-human captives exist as raw material — for labor, for experimentation, for eventual hollowing under controlled conditions.

**The experiments:** The rulers have begun systematic investigation of hollowing. They're forcing humans to cast under varying circumstances to observe outcomes. They want to understand:
- Can hollowing be directed toward specific drives?
- Can the process be controlled — stopped partway, reversed, accelerated?
- What happens to the magic stored in a person's soul? (They may be approaching philosopher's stone territory, though they almost certainly haven't arrived there yet)
- Can hollowed beings be given new drives after the fact?

**The military standstill:** On raw numbers and magical firepower, the Kingdom should win. They have tireless hollowed soldiers and aggressive, unrestrained casting. The Enclave's militia is 60-80 part-timers with improvised equipment. But the Kingdom has encountered something in battle that they cannot explain or counter.

When the Enclave fights defensively — their community praying behind the lines — their soldiers are *different*. Stronger, more enduring, resistant to hostile magic in ways that don't follow any pattern the Kingdom's rulers understand. The Kingdom has pushed into Enclave territory and been repelled by fighters who should have broken but didn't. Something is sustaining them. Something the Kingdom can't replicate.

The Kingdom's rulers are intelligent enough to recognize this as a genuine phenomenon, not morale or luck. They've observed it repeatedly. Their hollowed soldiers are immune to fatigue but not to whatever the Enclave is doing — the defensive effect blunts their attacks, dampens their magic, and turns engagements that should be decisive victories into grinding stalemates.

**The miracle problem:** The rulers have tried to reproduce the effect. They've forced hollowed servants to chant, to synchronize, to mimic the forms of collective action. Nothing happens. The mechanism requires authentic collective emotion — genuine shared feeling among souls acting as one — and hollowed beings are incapable of producing this. The rulers' empathy is gone. The servants' will is gone. Neither can generate the emotional density that makes collective resonance work. The Kingdom's greatest strength (hollowed obedience) is precisely what makes them unable to access the Enclave's greatest defense.

This is the primary driver of the Kingdom's strategic patience. They're not waiting because they lack force. They're waiting because the miracle is an unsolved variable. Their experiments — on hollowing, on soul mechanics, on magical manipulation — are partly motivated by the need to find a counter. If they can crack the mechanism behind the Enclave's "divine protection," the standstill ends overnight.

## What They Want

### Surface desires (what the missionaries can describe):
- Territory — land with deep mana pools to fuel their casting
- New spells — kanji and pairings they haven't discovered
- Subjugation — more people under their control
- Expansion — always more

### Hidden motivations (what nobody outside knows yet):
- **Human experimentation** — forcing captives to cast under controlled conditions, observing hollowing outcomes, treating people as lab rats for magical research
- **Engineered hollowing** — deliberately producing specific types of corrupted beings for specific purposes (soldiers, workers, scouts)
- **Soul extraction research** — they may be approaching the theoretical framework for philosopher's stones without having the concept named. They know souls contain dense magic. They're trying to figure out how to access it.
- **Solving the miracle** — the Enclave's collective prayer produces a defensive effect the Kingdom can't counter or replicate. Understanding and neutralizing this is a primary research goal, though they don't know the mechanism is collective emotional resonance.
- **Strategic patience** — they're not expanding because they can't crack the Enclave's defense. They're waiting for a breakthrough — in hollowing engineering, soul mechanics, or miracle-countering — that makes expansion inevitable.

## What Makes Them Terrifying

They're not monsters who forgot they were human. They're humans who decided other humans weren't. The hollowing didn't make them cruel — it removed the parts that would have stopped the cruelty that was always latent in their hierarchy. Their intelligence survived. Their organizational capacity survived. Their ambition survived and expanded to fill every void.

They represent the far end of the corruption spectrum: not the feral, bestial creatures born from simple desperation (like the village attackers), but the complex, intelligent corrupted beings that emerge when the dominant drives are sophisticated enough to sustain a functioning mind. A tragedy with teeth, a functioning society, and a research program.
