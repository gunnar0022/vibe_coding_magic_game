# SENNA — Community Anchor

## Core Role

Senna is the reason the player stays. Not through argument — through need. She's the face of a village that's lost its center and is looking to whoever's still standing. Her pitch isn't a sales job. It's a reality: if you leave, who's left?

---

## Who She Was Before

Senna is close to the player's age — old enough to carry responsibility, young enough that she shouldn't have to carry this much. She grew up in the village alongside the player, the brother, and Kael.

Her father was the closest thing the village had to a chief. Not a title anyone voted on — just the person everyone looked to.

Senna was his shadow. She'd been helping him since she was old enough to carry a message across the village. By her teens, she was doing half the work.

She was the older sister the whole village shared.

She had no children of her own. She didn't need any. She had the whole village.

---

## What She Lost

Her father died in the attack.

Senna wasn't with him when it happened. She was across the village, pulling children into shelter — protect the ones who can't protect themselves. She did everything right. She saved lives. And while she was saving them, her father was killed.

She didn't get to say goodbye. She didn't get to be there. She was doing his work in one part of the village while he died doing his work in another.

---

## What Happened After

Nobody appointed her. Nobody asked. Her father was gone, and the village needed someone who knew how things worked.

So she stepped into it. Not with confidence. Not with a sense of rising to the occasion. With the nauseating feeling of putting on shoes that don't fit.
She's filling her father's role because nobody else can.

The village doesn't see this. The village sees someone who stepped up.

What they don't see is that the work is the only thing between Senna and the grief underneath. She hasn't processed her father's death.

---

## Her Relationship to the Player

Senna and the player share a history — they grew up in the same village, know the same people, lost the same things. 

When the brother announces he's leaving, Senna feels it like a second attack. Another person walking away. Another absence the village has to absorb. She doesn't blame him — or she tries not to. 

Her appeal to the player isn't a speech. It's not calculated. It's the accumulated weight of everything she's been holding, slipping out in a quiet moment.

*"I can't do this alone. I know you need to make your own choice. But I need you to know that if you go, I'm still here. And it's getting harder."*

She doesn't say "you owe the village." She doesn't guilt the player explicitly. She just tells the truth: she's drowning, slowly, and she's too proud and too needed to say it to anyone except someone she trusts. 

That vulnerability is her real pull. Not obligation. Not duty. The simple human fact that someone you've known your whole life is asking for help and means it.

---

## If the Player Invests in the Village

Rebuilding alongside Senna isn't about following orders. It's two people sharing a burden that was designed for someone older and wiser than either of them.

The player becomes Senna's partner in the work, the person who takes half the weight so she can breathe. If this path is chosen, I want there to be cool features that unfold in the village like a sandbox. Maybe if the player maxes this path, it can lead to the unification of the tribes around the crossroads. They might migrate to the crossroads and build the first real town/city (that they know of) since the collapse.

Over time, the player can develop a strong connection with the community. 

The player who stays watches Senna go from someone surviving to someone living. The same goes for the rest of the village. 

This is the community storyline's emotional core: not building walls and growing crops, but being present for someone who needed you to stay, and finding that staying built something worth having.

---

## If the Player Leaves

Senna doesn't stop. She can't. The village still needs someone, and she's still the only one who knows how everything fits together.

But the weight doesn't distribute. It concentrates. The player's absence is one more thing Senna absorbs without complaint, one more gap she fills by working harder, sleeping less, sitting with her grief even less than before. The village survives — Senna makes sure of it — but Senna herself erodes.

The player might hear about the village through travelers. "That settlement with the young woman running everything. She looks tired." Or the player might return to find a village that functions but feels brittle — held together by someone who has been holding too tight for too long.

Senna won't say she's angry. She might not even be angry. But there will be something in her eyes when the player returns — a distance that wasn't there before. Not resentment. Just the recognition that the player made a choice, and Senna lived with the consequences.

---

## Design Notes

**Senna is not a guilt trip.** Her appeal works because it's genuine, not because it's manipulative. 

**The father's absence is the wound, not the attack.** The attack killed people and destroyed buildings. 

**Senna's work-as-suppression mirrors the brother's journey-as-suppression.** Both are coping with loss by refusing to stop moving. The brother runs outward. Senna runs in place. 

**The older sister archetype, not the mother.** Senna is too young to be maternal about this. She's not nurturing the village from a position of wisdom.

## Senna's Emotional Arc

### Act 1 (Prologue/Early Game)
- Desperate, exhausted, holding on
- Grieving Kira fresh enough that it shapes every interaction
- The pitch to the player is a last act of hope

### Act 2 (Mid-Game)
- If the player stays: Gradually less desperate, more grounded, finding rhythm in rebuilding
- Realizing the player is choosing to stay, which changes everything
- Releasing the grip on obligation

### Act 3 (Late Game)
- Healed enough to be real. Still grieving (always will be), but not broken by it.
- Standing with the player at endgame as a peer, not a suppliant
- Ready to build something new, not just survive what's left
