# The Mentor Researcher (Senior Knowledge Figure)

**Name:** ELOTH

## Locked Endpoints

- Introduced early in the game through the companion researcher. Their teacher.
- Not already wise, but has theories about magic that tend to be correct.
- Works toward understanding through rigorous, careful effort.
- Guides player and companion researcher to collect notes and test conclusions.
- The collaborative body of work the player helps build is a core narrative investment.
- **In the corruption ending:** Refuses to share dangerous findings with a hollowed player and brother. Sees what they've become. The player and brother turn against him to take by force what they used to earn through partnership.

## NPC Summary Line

"Wisdom that sometimes becomes withholding. Caution that can look like cowardice to the impatient."

## Established Traits

- Cautious. Pure in intention. Just wants to know.
- Wants to explore and uncover the secrets of this new world.
- He and his student are doing the heavy lifting for humanity's understanding of magic.

## Flaw

- Wisdom that sometimes becomes withholding. Caution that can look like cowardice to the impatient.

## Narrative Function

- Mentor theorizes. Student and player collect and test. Understanding grows through the relationship, not through exposition.
- The corruption ending's betrayal is deeply personal — the player destroys something they helped create. Betrays someone who trusted them with what they knew.

## Name & Identity

**ELOTH** (mid-forties, quiet, meticulous, carries himself with the careful precision of someone who has learned to move through the world without disturbing it more than necessary)

## Personal History: Before the Research Camp

### The Fall & Its Aftermath (50 years prior)

Eloth was a young child when the world collapsed.

He doesn't remember much at all. He just knows it was very different. Unfortunately, his parents were thinkers, not survivalists. They made it for a while, grouped up with the original group of people forming the current community he lives in, and they met a fatal accident with wild animals much too early in their lives. 

### The Early Years (Age 5-50)

For his whole life, Eloth had curiosity in the back of his mind, but the demand of the new life was too steep to pursue it. There was no infostructure, no systems, and hardly any housing. 
He simply had to spend most his life working in fields, picking through the forest for foods, and making simple things with the resources available to serve the community. 
He was never sure when any change happened, since it was all gradual. But he's pretty sure that each year forward he reaches, the world comes to look a bit different than it did previously, to the point where he's not even sure what it used to look like as a child. 


### The Research Camp (Age 50-Present)

Once Eloth got up in his years, his expectation for manual labor went down. Also, the community seemed to reach a point of stabilization, allowing for more lax lifestyles, at least, when compared to the days of his youth. These more lax days as an older guy allowed for his innate curiosity of his childhood to bubble up once more. 


Eloth established a semi-permanent research camp a ways into an area of the forest generally considered to be safe. He had space to do little experiments, monitor behavior of the altered wildlife and plants more closely, and he had some quiet. This was before humans really got a hold of magic, but he was already getting an idea of what might be happening. 

Of course, from the standpoint he started at, it was more like a fish trying to understand the nature of the water surrounding it than a scientist looking at reactions in a tube. Not to say he discovered nothing. He was definitely having some ideas about a sort of field permeating the air. That thing might be responsible for the strange behavior of the world. It seemed it must be stronger now than when he was younger. He never remembers noting anything of the kind in his youth, but now, once noticed, it's hard to imagine not realizing it was there from the start. How many years did he spend with his eyes closed to this phenomenon? 

For the first four years, the work was quiet and abstract. Environmental measurements. Seasonal observations. Nothing major and no breakthroughs. He also had to make frequent trips back to his home village for supplies and such.

Then, about a year ago, everything changed.

People across the world began discovering, almost simultaneously, that they could *channel* the field through symbols. That the invisible thing Eloth had noticed was real, tangible, and responsive to human intention shaped through meaning. The symbols were the key to unlock the power, and the price paid was from the environment. That was pretty much the only thing clear. 

Overnight, Eloth's years of environmental research became the most relevant data in the world. He was quick to disseminate the information to his community. Around this time, a young lass from his community took a great interest in the work of discovery and insisted Eloth take her in as an apprentice. 

In the year since the discovery, Eloth has worked with with astonishing vigor. He and his student Venn have pivoted from abstract environmental research to direct magical investigation. His camp became the forefront of magical innovation and discovery. With an assistant now, he was able to spend most of his time absorbed in research and experiments, while his assistant could run errands. 

Over the past year, he's built:
- Dataset: very modest, no more than 6 spells.
- Relationships with travelers and researchers, more interested in what's happening than understanding more spells. Mostly because Eloth can't offer that yet. 
- A reputation

And one student: Venn, who attached herself to him three years ago as a naturalist apprentice—drawn to his conviction that the invisible field was real—and has stayed because he made wanting to understand feel like the most important thing in the world. When magic became real, their partnership became urgent.

## The Mentor-Student Relationship: Eloth & Venn


### The Dynamic Now

**Eloth sees in Venn:**
- The person he might have been if he hadn't lost his parents in his youth
- Someone with potential
- Someone worth protecting


**Venn sees in Eloth:**
- Someone to finally feed her curiosity and take it seriously
- The person she wants to convince that understanding is worth the risk
- A role model

**The Tension:**
- Eloth holds back dangerous findings.
- Venn wants to move faster. She sees the gap between what they could do and what Eloth allows, and it frustrates her.


**And yet:** Eloth loves her like he might have loved a daughter.

## The Introduction Scene: Player Meets Eloth


### The First Conversation

Eloth is cataloguing observations when the player arrives. He doesn't look up for a moment. Just finishes his notation. Then sets down the journal and regards the player with clear, patient attention.

```
ELOTH: "Venn said you were coming. She's enthusiastic. She thinks you'll solve problems we haven't yet encountered. I think she's optimistic. Which one of us is right depends entirely on you."

PLAYER: [response depends on how they engage]

ELOTH: "I have rules for collaboration. There are three of them:
First: We test everything. No assumption survives contact with observation.
Second: You ask before you experiment. I'm not here to stop you. I'm here to make sure you understand what you're risking before you risk it.
Third: We document. Knowledge dies in heads. It persists in records."

[pause]

ELOTH: "Venn breaks the second one regularly. The brother will break it immediately. You seem like you might actually listen. That would be refreshing."
```

This is Eloth's entire introduction. He's not warming or grandfatherly. He's direct. He's offering genuine partnership under specific conditions. He's sizing up whether the player is actually serious or just passing through.

### Eloth's Initial Assessment

Eloth makes quick decisions about people based on how they respond to being given clear constraints:
- Do they resist? (He respects resistance if it's thoughtful)
- Do they nod along while clearly not caring? (He'll test them later)
- Do they ask clarifying questions? (He loves this)
- Do they assume the rules are flexible if they prove useful? (This he *hates*)

The player's initial response to his three rules effectively determines Eloth's posture toward them for the early game. Respectful skepticism gets the full research. Performative agreement gets close observation. Attempts to negotiate get firm refusal.

## Eloth's Voice & Personality

**Speech Patterns:**
- Speaks slowly. Lets silence exist. Doesn't rush to fill pauses.
- Uses simple, direct language. Avoids flourish.
- Asks questions more often than he makes statements.
- When he does state something, it's usually because he's tested it enough to be certain.

**Physical Presence:**
- Moves carefully. Economy of motion.
- Hands are stained with ink and plant matter.
- Wears practical layers — research in the field is weather-dependent.
- Eyes are unusually present. He watches more than he speaks.

**Emotional Temperature:**
- Warm but not effusive. Genuine care expressed through investment in your thinking, not through emotional availability.
- Boundaries are clear. He's your mentor, not your friend, though friendship might emerge from collaboration.
- Humor is dry, rare, and lands like a small gift when it appears.

**Example dialogue:**
- "That's a good question. Are you asking because you want to know the answer, or because you want me to validate something you already believe?"
- "The difference between observation and assumption is patience. We have time. We'll wait."
- "Venn thinks fear is a problem to solve. I think fear is information. What is it telling you?"

## The "Most Dangerous Findings" (Corruption Ending Setup)

Eloth has discovered certain things about magic that he believes are genuinely too dangerous to distribute:

### What He Knows (But Doesn't Share)

1. **Hollowing can be induced deliberately.** It's not just an accidental consequence of drawing too much. With specific knowledge of kanji mechanics and emotional resonance, a person can *force* the hollowing state in themselves or others. This is how the Skelemancer was created—not as a monster, but as someone's solution to a problem. And the worse the problem, the more complete the hollowing, the more powerful the resulting entity.

2. **Corruption isn't binary.** There are states between "human" and "fully hollowed." Venn's despair research could unlock the ability to navigate that space deliberately—to become more than human by losing the human parts you want to shed. The brother could theoretically weaponize this. The player could stumble into it.

3. **The partnership he's built with Venn and the player *depends* on them accepting that some knowledge should remain hidden.** Not withheld forever, but held carefully until the person asking has proven they won't use it to hurt themselves or others. The moment that trust breaks—the moment they demand the dangerous findings *before* he trusts them to hold it—the partnership is over.

### Why He Doesn't Share

It's not that Eloth is afraid of knowledge. It's that he has *seen* what happens when people get tools before they get wisdom—and in the year since magic became usable, he's seen it happen with horrifying speed.

He watched the collapse take Kess and others because systems they depended on failed faster than anyone understood. That taught him caution as a general principle.

In the past year alone, he's watched settlements hollow themselves because people figured out how to cast before they figured out what casting costs. The Failed Settlement drew from themselves rather than wait for mana to regenerate—and became the creatures that attacked the player's village. The Skelemancer exists because concentrated despair organized magic into something terrible.

Eloth's withholding is not cowardice. It's a position born from a lifetime of watching people rush into what they don't understand, applied now to something more dangerous than anything he's encountered before: **Some knowledge is responsibility, not resource.** You don't get the blueprint until you've proven you understand the weight of building it.

## First Appearance: A Quiet Authority

When the player first meets Eloth, he should feel:
- Grounded (this man knows what he's doing)
- Challenging (he's not here to make you comfortable)
- Trustworthy (his caution comes from care, not control)
- Slightly intimidating (he sees you clearly, and he's evaluating whether you're worth the risk)

This is the man the player will later betray in the corruption ending. Make that betrayal cost.
