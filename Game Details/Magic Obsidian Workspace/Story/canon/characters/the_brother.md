# The Brother

**Name:** SAEL (pronounced "sale")

A name that means something like "grief-bearer" in the old language, though by post-collapse times nobody remembers that. It's just a name. It sounds solid. Real. It can be said gently or with force, and both feel true.

## Locked Endpoints

- Older brother of the player. Competent, warm, proud before the attack.
- Saves the player's life during the village attack — this is the foundational emotional moment.
- Cannot stay in the village after the attack. The village is a wound he has to walk away from.
- Wants the player to come with him. Makes this clear with real emotional weight. Won't wait. Leaves.
- **His departure — not the attack — is the true inciting incident.**

## The Hollowing Arc

-This Character will have a personal storyline adjacent to the Player's story. The player can intervene in different ways. The general trend with this character is that he will hollow eventually if nothing is done. 

---

## Before the Attack: The Reliable One

Sael is three years older than the player. That gap is important. It's just enough to make him not-quite-peer and not-quite-parent. He's old enough to have real competence (Father is teaching him everything about building). Young enough to find joy in things instead of just duty.

The player's relationship with Sael is fundamentally rooted in **trust**.
Before the attack, Sael is:

- **Quiet-confident.** Not showy about what he knows. Just does things well and moves on.
- **Warm with a backbone.** Genuinely cares about people. Also has clear opinions and isn't apologetic about them.
- **Proud of his role.** Works with Thorne on building. Practices magic with Elsa (lesser strength than the parents, but steady). Helps Mira with documentation sometimes. He's integrated into the village's functional structure and takes that seriously.
- **Protective.** The player realizes, years later, that Sael was always watching out for them. Not hovering. Just... present. Available.
- **Someone the player wants to be like.** On the surface, and definitely at the start, he's just that kind of role model. 

### Key Pre-Attack Moment: First Steps

The Player and the Brother need to share moment before the game becomes chaotic. The brother should take the player aside in the evening and teach them their first spell. This should introduce the mechanic of spell casting as a tutorial. The brother will explain simple functional things, like, focusing on a certain image will allow you to produce magic. I think after sharing with the player, and the player casts their first magic, they should both be very surprised. It's not clear if the player has an affinity for magic like the brother? but really underneath, getting the magic for the first time is difficult, sharing it is trivial. This moment will develop the unknown about magic with the player and their character. 

## The Attack: Sael's Transformation

During the attack, Sael does three things that matter:

1. **Does not shut down**. He keeps a level head and fights. 
2. **Saves the player.** A moment when the player is in imminent danger, but the brother saves them. 
3. **Leaves.** He must leave the village. The player knows he doesn't want to be alone, but doesn't want to insist on the player coming along. 

Something breaks silently in the brother after the attack. Or rather, something locks into place. Some choice gets made that can't be unmade. Whatever happened, it's not obvious on the outside. On the inside, he's much more traumatized by the loss than he lets on, and pushes it down. He's incredibly anxious when thinking about anyone he still cares about, and is afraid to get too close to others he could lose again. He seeks to be stronger so one day he can live with confidence that anyone he cares for, he can also protect. 

## The Aftermath: Sael's Arc Begins

From this moment on, Sael's hollowing arc is set in motion.

He doesn't deliberately drain himself. He fights dangerous things to test and push his strength. He pushes himself harder because pushing is what keeps the grief from consuming him. This behavior leads to him casting after an area has been depleted. Slowly, this carves away at his own reserves. He doesn't notice. Even if he did, his actions might not change.
