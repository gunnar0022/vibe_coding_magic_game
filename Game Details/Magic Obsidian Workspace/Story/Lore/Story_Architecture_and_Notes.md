## CORE LORE PRINCIPLES

### Magic and the World
Magic entered the world suddenly and catastrophically. Technology failed instantly and permanently. Society collapsed overnight. Many died. Others fled to the wild.

By the time of the game — two to three generations later — magic is simply *normal*. The protagonist was born into it. It's not miraculous or feared, just part of the landscape. The world has stabilized into loose villages, isolated communities, and fragmented knowledge. Long-distance communication is rare. Travel is dangerous. Many don't know where their nearest neighbors are.

Magic appears to have organized spontaneously into something resembling a primitive form of life. It doesn't reason or judge. It responds. When invoked, it amplifies what already exists within living things, pushing them toward their natural extremes. Discovery is ongoing and global. No single community has complete understanding. What one village treats as foundational truth, another dismisses as superstition.

### How Magic Works (The Giving and Taking)
Every area in the world contains a large, shared pool of environmental mana, generated by the life in that region. All magic users — player and enemies — draw from this same pool. **Using magic from a full environment is safe.** The world gives freely when it has something to give.

The pool is finite but regenerates. Life breathes it back. The land always recovers. But the pool can be drained faster than it recovers, especially when multiple users draw from it simultaneously or when someone casts relentlessly without pause.

**The danger begins when you keep taking after there's nothing left.** When the environment is drained and you continue casting, you draw from yourself — your health, your being. Health-to-mana conversion is always available, consistent, and designed to feel convenient. The game never makes it feel desperate in the moment. That's what makes it dangerous. The cost accumulates invisibly.

### Corruption: The Hollowing
Magic itself is not the poison. Corruption begins when you take from yourself. Each time you cast from health — from your own being — it carves a gap in your soul. That gap is filled with *nothing*. Emptiness.

**That emptiness gets filled by whatever is loudest inside you.** Grief, pride, hunger, fear, wrath, ambition — whatever your dominant drives are rush in to fill the hollow space. This is the amplification. Magic doesn't inject corruption. It creates the void. Your own nature fills it.

The process is gradual. Invisible. You don't feel it happening. You just become *more* of what you already were, and less of everything else. The mana bar isn't just a resource tracker — it's the world telling you to stop. To wait. To let it breathe. The player who respects that boundary is safe. The player who pushes past it, spending health to keep casting, is choosing to hollow themselves out. The game doesn't punish you for using magic. It punishes you for refusing to stop.

### The Corruption Spectrum
The complexity of the transformation mirrors the complexity of the motive that fills the void.

A desperate, hungry group that hollowed themselves out trying to survive becomes something simple — feral humanoid creatures with one drive, one purpose, stripped of everything else. Bestial. Easy to recognize. Openly hostile. These are the remnants of simple desperation.

A person carrying grief, pride, protectiveness, wrath, and genuine love — if they hollow enough of themselves out, those drives expand to fill the space but retain their complexity. The result can still think. Still plan. Still feel twisted versions of what it felt before. Not a monster — a tragedy with teeth. The most dangerous corrupted beings in this world are the ones that can still talk to you.

The seven deadly sins work as a design lens, not a literal in-game system. The dominant drive determines the flavor of transformation. Hunger becomes gluttony. Fear becomes something that hoards and hides. Ambition becomes something that can never stop acquiring. Pride becomes something that must dominate. More complex characters, holding multiple drives simultaneously, produce more complex and more terrifying results.

### The World Is Regenerative. People Break.
There is no "spreading darkness" across the map. Drained areas recover as life breathes mana back. Corruption zones are temporary. The land always heals.

The danger is entirely behavioral. Settlements don't fall because corruption spread to them — they fall because people made choices under pressure and refused to stop taking. The land under them recovered after they were gone. The world is fundamentally generous. The tragedy is always human.

## THE VILLAGE AND THE ATTACK

The village is humble. About fifteen families. They farm, they hunt, they share. No currency — only cooperation. There's a central plaza where people eat together, a city hall type gathering space. It's small and it works. It's home.

The attack is coordinated. Altered humanoid creatures — corrupted humans, though the player doesn't know this yet — assault the village in force. They came from a nearby settlement that failed. Ran out of resources. Kept taking from themselves. Hollowed out. Simple desperation produced simple, bestial forms. Finding the origin settlement later is optional environmental storytelling, not a major questline.

The attack kills the protagonist's parents and childhood friend. About a quarter of the village is lost — lives, structures, resources. But the village survives. It's wounded, not destroyed. This matters: the community path has weight because there's actually something to rebuild.

**During the attack, the brother saves the player's life.** A clear, visceral moment. We see he's strong. We see he cares. This is the foundation everything else rests on.

The attackers are the endpoint of the brother's potential arc. The player has already seen the destination before the journey begins. They won't realize it until later — maybe not until late in the game, when they finally understand what hollowing does and look back at those first creatures with new eyes.

## THE BROTHER

Before the attack, he was reliable. Excited about life. A bit dignified — proud of the praise he received from their parents. He cared about his role in the family and the village. He was a good older brother.

After the attack, everything that made him warm gets channeled into intensity. He's consumed by grief, but it comes out as determination. Stubbornness. A sense of duty he can't articulate but can't ignore. The village is only bad memories now — a daily reminder of what was taken. He can't stay. He must go.

He wants the player to come with him. He makes this clear, and there's real emotional weight behind it — shared pain, shared loss, the fact that they're all each other has left. "We lost the same people. Don't you want to make sure this never happens again?" This isn't manipulation in the calculated sense. It's real love expressed through the need for control, the need for someone to validate his response by joining him. But it is leverage, and the player feels it.

He won't wait. He leaves.

**The brother's departure — not the attack — is the true inciting incident.** The attack creates the conditions. His leaving creates the choice.

### The Brother's Corruption
He's not hollowing because he fights too much or casts too many spells. He's hollowing because he's *relentless*. He pushes into drained areas and keeps casting. He doesn't rest. He doesn't wait for the world to recover. He takes from himself because waiting feels like surrender, and every gap he carves gets filled with the grief and wrath and pride that were always underneath his righteousness.

The insidious part: he's acting noble. Purging evil. Protecting people. The magic doesn't care about his justifications. It reads what's actually driving him. So he fights corrupted beings for months and feels righteous the whole time, while the hollowing accumulates beneath the surface. The gap between his self-image and his actual state grows invisibly until the moment it collapses.

On active paths, when the player walks with him, he seems *fine* at first. Maybe intense. Maybe a little harder-edged than they remember. But not obviously falling. The realization comes gradually — the way it does watching a loved one change in real life. You don't notice it day to day. You notice when you compare now to six months ago.

## THE 4-COMPASS SOUL LEVEL SYSTEM

### Overview
The game does not use traditional XP. Progression is handled through soul level — an overall value for gates and barriers, composed of four correlated sub-values representing the player's orientation.

### The Four Compass Points

**STRENGTH / POWER** — *"Nothing will take from me again."*
Combat mastery, monster-hunting, power-focused magic. The brother's preferred path. He always suggests this. **Choosing power = choosing the brother.** He IS this path — there is no separate power NPC.
*Corruption risk: Highest.* This path pushes hardest, drains most, rests least.

**KNOWLEDGE / UNDERSTANDING** — *"I'll find out why."*
Ruins, scholars, lore discovery. Guided by the mentor-student research chain. The brother agrees to accompany these quests — field experience may yield strength. Creates a party of three (player + researcher + brother).
*Corruption risk: Moderate.* Field work drains mana, but the mentor's caution provides a natural check.

**COMMUNITY / ROOTEDNESS** — *"This is home. I'll stay."*
Village rebuilding, farming, resource management, cooperation. Takes place in the home village — the player chooses to stay where it happened. The brother refuses to join. He can't sit still. He can't rebuild what was lost. The village is pain.
*Corruption risk: Very low.* Surrounded by living village generating ambient mana. Magic comes from a healthy environment. Almost never need to take from yourself.

**WANDERING / DETACHMENT** — *"I let go."*
Nomadic exploration, meditation, nonviolence. Moving through the world without agenda. The brother refuses to join — this path abandons him entirely.
*Corruption risk: Very low.* Moving through untouched landscapes, never draining any one place. Magic through meditation — the world offering, not the player taking.

### The Two Coins
The active coin is Strength vs. Knowledge. Both lead to learning more magic and getting stronger. The difference is framing, tone, the allies encountered, and how the world perceives the player's actions. The brother participates in both. Both carry real corruption exposure.

The passive coin is Community vs. Wandering. Both initially avoid direct confrontation, but the world pulls the player in regardless — the farmer needs ore from dangerous caves, the wanderer stumbles into corrupted territory. Both result in the brother going alone. Community says "I'm here if you come back." Wandering says "I've moved on." Both are mechanically safer — not because pacifism is rewarded, but because the lifestyle doesn't create the void. Less hollowing, less risk.

### Path-Specific Magic
Active paths learn magic through discovery and combat — direct, combative applications. Passive paths receive magic through meditation, distilled from the world. These spells serve real utility purposes rather than combative ones. Clever players may find tricky combat applications through ingenuity, but that's player creativity, not design intent.

### The Compass Is Hidden
The four sub-values are **never displayed to the player.** No stat screen, no button, no meter. This prevents optimization-driven play and preserves honest, instinctive choices. The player reads their orientation through observable world behavior: how NPCs treat them, what the brother says, what quests become available, how the village responds to their return. The game communicates through consequences, not numbers.

Cross-referencing matters: the brother's dialogue and reactions check the player's compass state. Trying to argue him off the path of hate when your own power value exceeds your understanding or community values will ring hollow. He notices the hypocrisy. A hollowed player telling the brother to show restraint is something the world reacts to with appropriate skepticism.

## THE NPCs

Each NPC's pitch is convincing from their perspective and genuinely flawed from the others'. No one is lying. Everyone is incomplete.

### The Researcher [Name TBD] — Knowledge Path Companion
Student of the Mentor Researcher. Excited, friendly, overhypes findings. Where the brother says "we need to be stronger," the researcher says "do you realize what this *means*?" They make the world feel fascinating instead of threatening. Their curiosity is genuine and infectious.

Their flaw is blind spots about cost. So excited by discovery that danger becomes secondary. They push forward where the mentor holds back. The tension between student and teacher is generative on the knowledge path — the player is positioned between caution and curiosity.

On knowledge quests, the brother tags along, creating a three-way dynamic: the brother's intensity, the researcher's enthusiasm, and the player between them. This overlap is where some of the game's richest NPC interactions live.

### The Mentor Researcher [Name TBD] — Senior Knowledge Figure
Introduced early in the game — not as a quest giver, but as someone who matters to the companion researcher. Their teacher. Possibly a father figure or actual parent. He's not already wise, but he has theories about magic that tend to be correct, and he works toward understanding through rigorous, careful effort.

He guides the player and the companion researcher to collect notes and test conclusions. Throughout the game, the contributions of student and teacher accumulate into a structured understanding of the world — **a collaborative body of work the player actively helps build.** Mentor theorizes. Student and player collect and test. Understanding grows through the relationship, not through exposition.

The mentor is cautious. Pure in intention. He just wants to know. He wants to explore and uncover the secrets of this new world. He and his student are doing the heavy lifting for humanity's understanding of magic.

**In the corruption ending:** This is the person who refuses to share his most dangerous findings with a hollowed player and brother. He sees what they've become. He knows what they'll do with the knowledge. And the player — who helped gather the data, who contributed to the research, who earned trust through collaboration — turns against him to take by force what they used to earn through partnership. It's not attacking a stranger. It's destroying a project you helped build. Betraying someone who trusted you with what they knew, because you decided they were wrong to hold back the rest.

### The Community NPC [Name TBD] — Community/Farming Path
Desperate not to lose anyone else. Their pitch is obligation and belonging — "If you leave, who's left?" The village genuinely needs help. These are real people who will suffer if everyone capable leaves. The pull of home is real.

Their flaw is that their need can trap the player. Staying because someone begs you to isn't the same as choosing to stay. Obligation can masquerade as belonging. But the need is legitimate, and the community they offer is genuine. The player who stays finds something real — roots, cooperation, possibly love (spouse mechanics). The cost is everything happening beyond the village's borders, including the brother's descent.

### The Wanderer/Mentor [Name TBD] — Wandering/Detachment Path
Not a walking companion. A presence found at destinations — someone the player encounters at the places they arrive, not someone who walks alongside them. A recurring figure who shares wisdom about their own journey, but never stands beside you the way other NPCs do.

They've lost everything already. Found peace in movement. Calm where everyone else is frantic. They've seen what the other paths lead to, and they advise nonviolence and disconnection with the quiet certainty of someone who's been through it. From their detached perspective, even losing the brother is inevitable — "the way the wind blows." Their cryptic advice is grounded in real experience, not mysticism.

Their flaw is that they've stopped caring enough to act. Their peace was bought by abandoning responsibility. They might be right about the danger — but being right doesn't help the people who needed them. The world they guide you through is genuinely beautiful. The question is whether beauty is enough when the people you loved are still out there breaking.

### NPC Summary
- **The Brother:** Real love expressed through control. The need for validation disguised as protection.
- **The Researcher:** Genuine passion with blind spots about cost. Curiosity that forgets to check for danger.
- **The Mentor Researcher:** Wisdom that sometimes becomes withholding. Caution that can look like cowardice to the impatient.
- **The Community NPC:** Legitimate need that can become a cage. Belonging that might be obligation in disguise.
- **The Wanderer:** Hard-won peace bought by abandoning everyone who needed them. Right about the danger, absent for the fight.

## STRUCTURAL COHESION: ONE GAME, NOT FOUR

### The Shared Emotional Question
**"What do you do with loss?"**

Power says: "Prevent it." Knowledge says: "Understand it." Community says: "Rebuild from it." Wandering says: "Accept it."

The brother embodies the question in its rawest form. His fate is the measure of the player's answer. This question is present in every path, every act, every interaction. It's what makes the game feel unified regardless of which compass direction the player leans.

### Four Layers of Cohesion

**LAYER 1: WORLD EVENTS (Shared Reality)**
The world operates on a timeline independent of the player's path. The same things happen out there — settlements fall, mana zones deplete, factions rise, the brother acts. What changes is which parts of those events the player witnesses firsthand, hears secondhand, or misses entirely.

*Example — a settlement falls to corruption:*
The power player fights what came out of it. The researcher walks through it and understands *why* it fell. The farmer deals with refugees arriving at the village. The wanderer finds the ruins months later, overgrown and quiet. Same event. Four angles. One world. None is "the real version." All are partial.

**LAYER 2: SHARED VERBS (Mechanical Identity)**
Every path uses the same core mechanics: casting magic, exploring spaces, managing mana, interacting with NPCs, surviving encounters. The farmer fights wolves threatening crops with the same magic system the power player uses against corrupted beings. The wanderer navigates dangerous terrain with the same stamina and resource management. The context for using mechanics changes. The mechanics themselves don't.

**LAYER 3: THE BROTHER AS THROUGHLINE**
No matter what path the player takes, the brother's arc is happening. The power player walks beside him. The researcher encounters him at the same sites. The farmer hears about him from travelers returning to the village. The wanderer finds traces of his passage in distant places. He's the one story thread every version of the game shares, experienced differently but always present. His fate is the emotional constant.

**LAYER 4: THE CROSSROADS (Shared Space)**
No large hub town exists — that would contradict the lore of isolated, post-collapse communities and flatten the diversity of small villages with their own cultures and superstitions. Instead, a *crossroads*: a meeting place that emerged naturally from geography. A river crossing, a mountain pass, a junction of old roads. People don't live there permanently. It's a liminal space — a periodic bazaar or seasonal gathering point where different villages send people for trade and information.

The brother passes through because everyone does eventually. The farmer trades there. The researcher finds scholars passing through. The wanderer rests. Same place, different reasons, different NPCs foregrounded. It preserves the isolation of individual communities while giving the paths a natural point of intersection. Not a safe haven. Not governed. Just geography forcing people to overlap.

### Why Mixed-Path Players Don't Break It
A player who switches emphasis mid-game doesn't break the story — they shift their angle on the same unfolding reality. Because world events happen regardless, the player who samples multiple paths gets a broader but shallower picture. The player who commits deeply gets a narrower but richer one. Both are valid.

## STORY ARCHITECTURE: ACT STRUCTURE

### PROLOGUE / TUTORIAL — "The Village"

The player wakes in the village. Daily life. The brother teaches a symbol or two — this is the mechanical tutorial, but it's also a relationship. We see the brother as competent, warm, proud. A good older sibling. The player interacts with parents and the childhood friend. These need to be *specific people* — not narrative fuel, not archetypes. The player should remember something about each of them.

Small tasks using basic magic. Magic as mundane, practical, part of life. Duration: about 20-30 minutes. Enough to feel the loss coming, not so long it drags.

**The attack.** Coordinated. Terrifying. Altered humanoid creatures pour into the village. The brother fights. **He saves the player's life** — a clear, visceral moment that establishes everything the player needs to feel about him. Parents die. The childhood friend dies. The village is torn but standing.

**The departure.** Aftermath. Grief. The brother can't stay. The village is a wound he has to walk away from. He wants the player to come — makes this clear with the weight of everything they've both lost behind it. But he won't wait. He leaves, driven by a duty he can't name and a pain he can't sit with.

The player is left in a damaged but living village. The four compass NPCs begin to emerge in the margins — people with their own responses to what happened, their own ideas about what comes next. The pull in each direction becomes felt. This is the fork. Not a dialogue menu. A feeling.

### ACT ONE — "The First Steps"

The player encounters opportunities aligned with all four compass directions. Early quests introduce each NPC naturally — the researcher passing through with excitement about a discovery, the community NPC organizing rebuilding efforts, the wanderer encountered at the edge of known territory. The brother is already gone, but NPCs mention him. He passed through a neighboring settlement. He was intense. He asked questions no one could answer. He left quickly.

First real exploration beyond home (or deep investment in home, for the community path). The environmental mana system becomes apparent — the realization that the bar isn't "yours" begins here. First encounters with other communities, each with their own interpretation of magic, their own superstitions and certainties.

The mentor researcher is introduced — not as a quest giver, but through the companion researcher. Their teacher. Someone who matters to someone the player is starting to care about. The knowledge chain begins to form.

First soul level milestone. Non-combat — a discovery, a connection made, a symbol understood in a new way.

### ACT TWO — "The Widening World"

**The quest fork structure.** At major decision points throughout Act 2, multiple quests are available simultaneously — one for each compass orientation. The player can only choose one per fork. This is where sacrifice lives.

Active quests (power/knowledge) may result in encountering the brother, who is pursuing similar objectives in the same regions. These reunions aren't forced — they're natural overlaps. The brother is doing his own thing, and sometimes the player's path crosses his because they're chasing the same answers or hunting the same threats. In these shared moments, the player's compass state influences dialogue. The brother's mood is shaped by cumulative choices — how many active forks brought the player alongside him versus how many passive forks left him alone.

Passive quests (community/wandering) mean the brother moves on alone for that milestone. His hollowing advances without the player's influence. Choosing to farm when there's a battle to fight, choosing to wander when there's a lead to follow — these are real sacrifices with real consequences for someone who isn't there.

**The brother on active paths:** He seems fine at first. Intense, yes. Driven. But the player who walks with him through Act 2 watches a gradual change — not dramatic, not marked by a single moment, but visible in retrospect. He's a little harder each time they meet. A little less willing to rest. A little more certain that the only answer is more force. The hollowing is happening, but it looks like determination. It looks like strength. It looks like someone who's handling it.

**Understanding corruption:** There is no single quest that delivers the full picture. Instead, puzzle pieces accumulate at different rates depending on the path. The researcher and mentor's collaborative work provides structured understanding on the knowledge path. The power player witnesses corruption firsthand but may lack the framework to interpret it. The farmer hears stories from travelers. The wanderer encounters evidence in quiet, abandoned places.

By mid-Act 2, an active-path player should understand enough about the hollowing to make any further steps toward corruption a deliberate, informed choice. A passive-path player may not fully understand until the consequences arrive in Act 3. This asymmetry is intentional — you learn about dangers by facing them, not by avoiding them.

**World events unfold regardless of path.** Settlements struggle. Mana scarcity begins to matter — dead zones appear, environmental mana doesn't fully recover between visits, and the cost of magic becomes tangible. Encounters with corrupted beings become more varied: some bestial and simple, others disturbingly clever, others almost human in their cruelty. NPC stories about "those who went too far" accumulate — not lectures, just stories. Context that the player files away until it suddenly applies to someone they care about.

### ACT TWO TURNING POINT — [NEEDS DEVELOPMENT]

The hallucination quest has been shelved (see Shelved Ideas). What replaces it must accomplish:
- Recontextualize the player's understanding of magic's true cost — the hollowing, the void, what fills it.
- Raise the stakes for the brother's trajectory specifically.
- Ensure enough understanding that further corruption is a deliberate choice, not an accident.
- Work as a convergence of accumulated understanding, not a single dramatic reveal.

Possible directions to workshop:
- A discovery that reveals the village attackers were people — were hollowed — and what drove them to that point. Not a cure. Not a reversal. Just understanding that hits differently now.
- An encounter with someone mid-hollowing who is still lucid enough to describe what's happening to them. A mirror. A preview. Not a teaching moment.
- A moment where the brother does something that scares the player — or vice versa — forcing both to confront what they're becoming.
- The revelation that mana depletion drives hollowing at a civilizational scale. Systemic understanding, not personal drama.

### ACT THREE — "Convergence"

The brother's state is determined by the cumulative weight of every fork the player chose across Acts 1 and 2. How many times did you walk beside him? What was your own compass state when you did? How many times did you leave him alone? The answer shapes which ending the game moves toward.

---

**COMMUNITY / ROOTEDNESS — "I stayed. I built. And he broke alone."**

The player chose peace through building. The village is stronger for it — thriving, even. There may be deep relationships, a spouse, a real community forged from rubble. The player answered the question of loss by rebuilding from it, and the answer worked. For them.

But the brother was left alone. He spiraled. He hollowed. He became a corrupted threat — possibly the most complex and dangerous one the player has faced, because his motives were never simple. He wasn't driven by hunger or fear. He was driven by grief and love and wrath and pride, all tangled together, and the thing he became reflects all of it.

The player must confront what their choice cost. Not a villain — a failure of absence. The tragic irony: by building peace, by staying home, by choosing not to perpetuate the cycle, you created the conditions for the brother's destruction. You didn't cause it. But you weren't there to prevent it.

**WANDERING / DETACHMENT — "I let go. I saw everything. I lost everyone."**

The player chose peace through release. They've traveled the world. Seen the most of it. If the game is filled with artistic and magical scenery, this player witnessed all of it. They found serenity — but at the cost of every tie they had.

The brother was fully abandoned. Consumed by hollowing, or perhaps simply vanished. The player may encounter evidence of his fate in some distant, quiet place. Or they may never know. The world doesn't give clean resolution to those who chose to let go.

The most ambiguous ending. Freedom means not knowing. The wanderer was right that other paths are dangerous. The question is whether being right matters when the people you loved needed you to be wrong and *present*.

**CONSOLATION (Knowledge-dominant, active path) — "I walked beside him. I understood enough to help."**

The player chose the knowledge path — walked with the brother on active quests, argued from a place of genuine understanding rather than matching his intensity with more intensity. The compass reflects knowledge and restraint rather than raw power. The brother saw the player experience the same pain and choose differently — not choosing weakness, but choosing to understand before acting.

The brother is at the edge, but not over it. The player's presence throughout Act 2 — consistent, patient, informed — is the counterweight. The culmination isn't a boss fight. It's a shared challenge that requires cooperation, trust, complementary magic. The brother sees the player choose not to destroy, and something in the pattern breaks.

Recovery isn't instant. It's not clean redemption. But it begins. The brother becomes a true, steadfast ally — not because the player saved him, but because they stood beside him long enough for him to save himself.

**CORRUPTION (Power-dominant, active path) — "We needed it. He wouldn't give it. So we took it."**

Both player and brother pursued power. Both let the hollowing accumulate. Both are deeply changed — but they're changed *together*, and that shared transformation has its own dark gravity.

The sealing moment: the mentor researcher — the person the player may have worked with, gathered data for, contributed to — has made great discoveries about the most potent and dangerous forms of magic. He sees what the player and brother have become. He recognizes the hollowing in them. He refuses to share his most dangerous findings, because he knows exactly what they'll do with them.

The player and brother decide to take him down and steal his notes. This is the act that can't be justified as noble. Not purging evil. Not protecting anyone. Attacking someone good because they have something you want. Someone who trusted you. Someone whose work you helped build. The magic has everything it needs. The void fills completely.

The game doesn't end. It *shifts*. The player and brother are now corrupted beings in a world that fears them. Community exists only among others who've hollowed out the same way. Power was gained — real, terrible power. But belonging was the price. They became the thing that attacked their village. And the game lets you keep playing in that reality, because the consequence isn't death. It's transformation. It's becoming something that survives but doesn't fit.

### EPILOGUE — "What Remains"

The world continues. It doesn't care about the player's arc. Communities grow, struggle, adapt. Magic remains mysterious, dangerous, beautiful. The land heals. People do what people do.

The player's legacy is defined by their choices — not explained by a narrator, not judged by the game. Open-ended. You decided what to do with loss. The world absorbed your answer and kept going.

## PATH-SPECIFIC TRADEOFFS

These are representative of life. You can't do everything. You can't please everyone. And it's not always bad.

- **Knowledge/Researcher:** Keeps the brother. Understands the world through hardship. Richest NPC relationships (researcher + mentor + brother). May have missed parts of the world. Has a larger community of contacts.
- **Strength/Power:** Keeps the brother. Becomes strong. But the costs are severe — hollowing, damage done, relationships strained. The path with the most power and the most risk.
- **Community/Farmer:** Gains deep roots. Village thrives. Potential spouse. Real belonging. But loses the brother in the harshest way — he spirals alone because you stayed.
- **Wandering/Detachment:** Sees the most. Experiences the most beauty. But loses the brother and the community left behind. Beautiful loneliness.

None should leave a bad taste. Each is a valid answer to loss, with real gains and real costs.

## LORE IMPLICATIONS TO TRACK

- **Hollowing is the unified theory.** Taking from yourself when there's nothing left creates the void. Your nature fills it. This connects corruption, the brother, the attackers, and the entire bestiary.
- The mana bar is a warning system. The world telling you when to stop.
- Passive paths are safer not because pacifism is rewarded, but because the lifestyle doesn't create the void. Less taking, less hollowing.
- The brother's corruption is invisible because his motives are genuinely mixed — love and wrath coexist, and the hollowing amplifies both without distinguishing between them.
- The mentor researcher's collaborative body of work makes the corruption ending's betrayal deeply personal — you're destroying something you helped create.
- The corruption spectrum means every monster the player fights was once a person who emptied themselves out. The simple ones were driven by simple needs. The complex ones had complex hearts.
- Passive-path magic (meditation, utility) mirrors the lore's "resonances" — the world aligning with perception, offering rather than being taken from.

## SHELVED IDEAS

### Hallucination / Major Corruption Quest
Player perceives allies as monstrous during a corruption crisis. Two resolution paths: accept defeat (corruption reduced) or kill allies (corruption deepens).

**Why shelved:** Conflicts with the mirror metaphor between player and brother. Creates a retroactive "could they have been saved?" problem with every corrupted entity the player has fought. Undermines the informed-commitment model — corruption should feel like a deliberate slide, not a sudden reveal.

**Could return as:** A late-game optional encounter for very high corruption players. Not a cure — just a moment of terrible clarity. No mechanical reversal. Just seeing yourself clearly for an instant before the void fills back in.
