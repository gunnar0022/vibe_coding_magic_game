# LORE SNIPPET — The Hollowing

## Core Principle

Magic itself is not the poison. Using magic from a full environment is safe. The world gives freely when it has something to give.

The hollowing begins when you take from yourself. When the environmental mana pool is drained and you continue casting, you draw from your own health — your own being. Each time you do this, it carves a gap inside you. That gap is filled with nothing. Emptiness.

That emptiness gets filled by whatever is loudest inside you. Grief, pride, hunger, fear, love, ambition — whatever your dominant drives are rush in to fill the hollow space. This is the amplification. Magic doesn't inject anything. It creates the void. Your own nature fills it.

The process is gradual. Invisible. You don't feel it happening. You just become *more* of what you already were, and less of everything else.

## Why It Feels Safe

Drawing from your health when the environment is empty is efficient. The conversion feels convenient, not desperate. In most situations, a person won't hesitate to trade a little vitality for one more spell. That convenience is what makes it dangerous.

The mana bar is not just a resource tracker. It is the world telling you to stop. The person who respects that boundary is safe. The person who pushes past it is choosing — whether they realize it or not — to hollow themselves out.

The world does not punish you for using magic. It punishes you for refusing to stop.

## The Spectrum

The hollowing is one process. What comes out the other side depends on what was loudest inside the person when the void formed. The complexity of the transformation mirrors the complexity of the motive.

**Simple drive hollowing.** A desperate, hungry group that hollowed themselves trying to survive becomes something simple — feral humanoid creatures with one drive, one purpose, stripped of everything else. Bestial. Easy to recognize. Openly hostile. The remnants of simple desperation. The village attackers are this. A failed settlement that ran out of resources, kept casting to survive, started drawing from themselves, and never stopped. Simple need, simple hollowing, simple creatures at the end.

**Complex drive hollowing.** A person carrying ambition, pride, intelligence, and the will to dominate — if they hollow enough of themselves out, those drives expand to fill the space but retain their complexity. The result can still think. Still plan. Still feel twisted versions of what it felt before. The most dangerous hollowed beings are the ones that can still talk to you. The Unholy Kingdom's rulers are this — leaders whose ambition and entitlement were amplified into something monstrous but calculating.

**Subjugated hollowing.** Those under the complex hollowed. People whose dominant drive was reduced to fear and compliance through domination *before* they hollowed. When they hollow, what fills the void is obedience. Emptied by their rulers rather than by their own choices. Hollowed servants — a category that shouldn't exist, but the Kingdom engineered it. The subjugation came first. The hollowing came second. What's left is a shell that follows orders because following orders is all that was loud enough to survive.

**Noble drive hollowing.** The hardest to recognize and the saddest to encounter. A person who hollows from love, devotion, protection, or the desperate need to save someone. The motive is genuinely good. The mechanics are the same. The void doesn't care *why* you took from yourself. It just fills with whatever was loudest.

A healer who drains themselves saving a friend doesn't become a monster. They become something that can only heal, only protect, only tend — obsessively, possessively, without restraint or judgment. The parts of them that provided perspective, boundaries, the ability to accept outcomes — those eroded. What's left is the drive without the wisdom that shaped it. Amplified love without limits becomes a cage. The friend is healed, but now the friend has a different problem: a person who used to be whole is now a hollow shell of devotion that won't let them leave, won't let them take risks, won't let anyone near them.

Noble hollowing doesn't produce evil. It produces tragedy. The system doesn't distinguish motive. Same void, same amplification, same loss. The nobility of the intention only shapes the *flavor* of what fills the emptiness. And the cost gets transferred — the hollowed person's suffering becomes their loved one's prison. The original problem wasn't solved. It was moved.

Early-stage noble hollowing is the hardest to detect, because it looks like devotion. Like someone who cares deeply. Like a really good person. The signs are subtle — they stop sleeping, stop eating, lose interest in everything except the object of their care. Friends might say "they're so dedicated" long past the point where it's become pathological.

## What Gets Lost

The hollowing doesn't add anything. It subtracts everything except what's loudest, and then amplifies what remains.

A person is made of contradictions. They're brave *and* afraid. Loving *and* selfish. Curious *and* cautious. These tensions are what make them human. The hollowing dissolves the tensions. It picks the loudest signal and turns up the volume until that's all there is. The feral creature is pure hunger. The tyrant is pure ambition. The devoted protector is pure love. And each of them is less than what they were, because a single drive — no matter how powerful — is a smaller thing than a whole person.

This is why the hollowed are tragic, not evil. They didn't choose to become less. They were carved away by a process they didn't understand, and what's left is genuinely *them* — just not all of them. The worst version of the best part of who they were.

## Coming Back

Life breathes everything back. The same principle applies to people — but slower, harder, and never cleanly.

A person who stops taking from themselves early enough can recover. The suppressed parts of who they were — the contradictions, the quieter drives, the complexity — aren't destroyed yet. They're drowned out. Given time, given a rich environment, given the willingness to stop and let the quiet parts breathe again, those pieces can reassert. The void doesn't refill the way a mana pool does. It's more like a wound that scars over. The person comes back, but not unchanged. There's an itch that stays — the pull toward the amplified drive, the memory of how simple and powerful it felt to be only one thing. Some days the scar is invisible. Some days it aches.

The further in, the harder the return. Past a certain depth, the other parts of you aren't suppressed anymore — they're gone. There's nothing left to regrow. The feral creature that was once a desperate farmer has no quiet voice underneath waiting to come back. The line between "scarred but recoverable" and "permanently lost" is blurry, and nobody knows exactly where it sits.

## The World Heals. People Break.

There is no "spreading darkness." Drained areas recover as life breathes mana back. The land always heals. The danger is entirely behavioral. Settlements don't fall because corruption spreads to them. They fall because people made choices under pressure and refused to stop taking. The land under them recovered after they were gone.

The world is fundamentally generous. The tragedy is always human.
